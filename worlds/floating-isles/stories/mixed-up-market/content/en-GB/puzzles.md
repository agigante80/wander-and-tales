# The Market of Mixed-Up Magic: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need
to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always
moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward cleverness and kindness
over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Muddled Stalls (Easy)

**The challenge.** Every object has jumped to the wrong stall. The players need a way
to discover where each thing truly belongs.

**Intended solutions.** Any of the magics can help:
- **Voice** is the natural fit: ask each object where its home is, and it will happily
  tell you (the boots want the cobbler, the bread wants the baker, and so on).
- **Light** reveals a faint matching mark or colour that pairs each object to its
  stall.
- **Change** can mend a torn label so a stall can be read again.

**Fallback.** If a roll misses, the Mist Cat picks up one object in its mouth and
carries it pointedly to the right stall, showing the children how, then they spend an
energy star or combine two magics to finish the easy ones. Nobody is stuck for long.

## Stop 2: The Memory of the Market (Normal)

**The challenge.** To return the rest, the players must remember the market as it was:
which goods belonged at which stall.

**Solution.** This is a **memory** moment. The four pairings are:
- bread goes to the **baker**
- fruit goes to the **greengrocer**
- shoes go to the **cobbler**
- toys go to the **toymaker**

The Talking Flower hums the stalls in order to help. Let the children recall and
place each pairing. Working it out with the flower's help still counts as a full
success.

**Fallback.** If they freeze, have the Talking Flower sing the order slowly, one stall
at a time, and let the players place one pairing per line. Getting there with a nudge
tops up an energy star.

## Stop 3: The Last Swap (Normal)

**The challenge.** Three stalls are tangled in a loop: the baker holds boots, the
cobbler holds a kite, and the toymaker holds bread. The players must work out the
passes that put everything right.

**Solution.** Each stall's own goods are: baker = bread, cobbler = shoes (boots),
toymaker = toys (the kite). So it is a three-step ring:
- the **baker** passes the boots to the **cobbler**,
- the **cobbler** passes the kite to the **toymaker**,
- the **toymaker** passes the bread to the **baker**.

Then everyone holds their own. Lay out three objects and three stalls so the children
can physically move them; the logic clicks once they see it as a ring.

**Fallback.** If they are stuck, the Mist Cat nudges the boots toward the cobbler to
start the chain ("boots belong with shoes"), and the rest tends to follow. Solving it
with one nudge is a full success.

## Pip the New Mage (no roll)

This is the resolution, not a puzzle, so there is no band and no roll. Behind a stall
the players find **Pip**, a kind apprentice mage on their very first day, whose Change
spell slipped and muddled the market, and who hid away out of worry and shyness.

Play it softly. Let the children decide how to reach out: a kind word, a reminder that
everyone makes mistakes, an offer to stand beside Pip while they try again. There is
no scolding and no trick. When the children reassure Pip, Pip finds the courage to set
the last thing right with a careful Change, and the market cheers. End on that warmth.
The children have won by being clever and kind, which is the only way anyone wins on
the Isles.
