# The Market of Mixed-Up Magic

*Read aloud, or let an older child read. Pause at the questions and let everyone
share their ideas. There are no wrong answers, only new ones to try.*

## Before you begin

Market day on the home island is the busiest, brightest morning of the week. All
along the Market Row the stalls are heaped high: warm loaves cooling on the baker's
counter, ripe fruit stacked in glowing pyramids, neat rows of shoes at the cobbler's,
and a whole rainbow of toys waiting to be wound up and played with.

Then comes a small flash, a soft pop, and a sound like the whole market drawing a
surprised breath. When it clears, everything has jumped to the wrong place. The baker
stands blinking at a pair of muddy boots. The cobbler turns a kite over in puzzled
hands. Fruit sits where toys should be, toys where bread should be, and not a single
stallholder can find a thing that belongs to them.

You are young mages, and a tangle like this is exactly the sort of thing you are
learning to mend. You share a quick look and roll up your sleeves. "Let us untangle
the market," you say, and the muddle waits for you.

*What is your mage name? Which magic feels most like you today: Light, Grow, Change,
or Voice?*

## Stop 1: The Muddled Stalls

You make your way down the row, and everywhere you look an object sits where it does
not belong, looking faintly bewildered. A pair of boots blinks up at you from among
the loaves, as if to ask how on earth it ended up there.

The Mist Cat pads out of the morning haze and settles at your side, soft and grey and
ready to lend a paw.

*How will you discover where each thing belongs? Could you simply ask the objects
themselves where their home is? Try something, and see what the market tells you.*

Once the objects begin to name their proper stalls, you set to work carrying them
home, and the easy ones are soon back in their places. But a handful are still
hopelessly jumbled, and for those you will need to think a little harder.

## Stop 2: The Memory of the Market

To untangle the rest, you must call up a picture of the market as it was before the
spell went wrong: which stall held which goods, and exactly where everything stood.
That is no small thing, with the whole row shuffled around you.

A Talking Flower, nodding in its pot on the corner, leans close and hums the market
back to you in order, stall by stall, like a little song you can lean on.

*Can you remember which stall sold the bread, which the fruit, which the shoes, and
which the toys? Match each muddled thing back to the stall it came from.*

As each thing returns to where it began, more and more of the Market Row settles into
happy order again. Only one stubborn knot is left, and it is the trickiest of them
all.

## Stop 3: The Last Swap

Three stalls remain tangled, and they are tangled in a ring. The baker is holding a
pair of boots, the cobbler is holding a kite, and the toymaker is holding a fresh
loaf of bread. Every one of them has something that belongs to one of the others, and
to set it right you must work out, carefully, who should pass which thing along to
whom.

*Who hands their thing to whom, so that in the end every stall has exactly what it
should? Reason it out together, one pass at a time.*

When the last swap comes untangled and each stall holds its own again, a cheer runs
the length of the Market Row. Everything is mended. Yet one question still hangs in
the air, unanswered: where did all this muddle begin?

## Pip the New Mage

Behind a quiet stall at the far end, you find the answer curled up small and trying
very hard to be invisible: a young apprentice mage, no older than you, with a pointed
hat sitting crooked over a worried face. Their name is Pip, and today is their very
first day of magic.

Pip, you learn, had only wanted to help. They had tried a small Change spell to tidy
a stall, and the spell had slipped and spun out across the whole market, swapping
everything as it went. Frightened of the muddle they had made, and far too shy to own
up, Pip had crept away to hide.

There is no scolding here, and there never was anything to fear. Everyone fumbles a
spell now and then, and a first day is exactly when it is most likely to happen.

*What do you say to Pip? How can you show them that all is well, that no one is cross,
and that nobody mended this market alone, you mended it together?*

When you tell Pip, gently and truly, that everything is alright, they uncurl and peep
out with the smallest of smiles. Then, steadier now, Pip lifts one careful Change and
sets the very last thing right, and the whole Market Row breaks into applause. Pip
straightens up and stands a little taller than before.

You did this together. You were kind, and you were full of ideas, and on the Isles that is
the only way anyone ever wins.
