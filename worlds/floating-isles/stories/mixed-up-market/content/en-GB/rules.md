# The Market of Mixed-Up Magic: Rules for the Grown-Up

*New to this? Read the Guide for the Grown-Up first. It explains the whole idea in
two pages and you will never need it again.*

You are the Game Master, which on the Isles means a few small jobs at once:
narrator, gentle referee, the children's biggest fan, and a fellow adventurer who
plays along. This is a cooperative game for two or more who play together, a grown-up
and one or more children, all on the same side. Nobody competes, and nobody loses.

## Setting up

- You do not need a printed map for this one. Picture the Market Row, or lay out a
  few household objects to be the stalls and their goods.
- Each player chooses one magic: Light, Grow, Change, or Voice. Two different magics
  work best, because combining them is half the fun. (Voice is especially handy here,
  but every magic has a place.)
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). These are spent
  and refilled during play.
- Build the muddled goods (a loaf, a pair of boots, a kite, some fruit) from
  household bits and bricks, so the children can move them about. Anything can be
  anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## Difficulty bands, not dice

Challenges never call for a particular die. Instead each one has a band: **Easy**,
**Normal**, or **Hard**. When the players try something, look up the band on the
kit's dice-band table, which tells you the number to meet or beat with whatever dice
your family owns. Every challenge in this story can be played with a single ordinary
die, and richer dice sets simply make it feel grander.

## The golden rule: here nobody loses

A failed roll is never a defeat. It is a detour to a different route. When a roll
does not meet the band, the players always have a way through:

- **Spend an energy star** to try the same idea again, or
- **Combine two magics** once per stop to pass the challenge with no roll at all
  (Voice and Light, say, or Change and Grow, working as one).

Energy stars come back when the players move on to the next stop, and when they help
each other. So nobody can ever run dry for long, and nobody is ever stuck.

## Helping each other

At any moment one player may help another instead of acting alone. When they do, make
the help roll: on a success the challenge is passed for the pair, even if the first
roll missed. Helping is generous, so reward it; it also tops up an energy star. The
lesson underneath the whole game is that you get further together.

## The surprise

At each stop, once, the players may roll for a surprise and you read out the matching
line. Use whichever die the dice-band table gives you for surprises; with a single
ordinary die, read the line that matches the number rolled.

| Roll | Surprise |
|---|---|
| 1 | A Messenger Butterfly flutters past and gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | A kind stallholder hands you a snack: take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A tidy little gust of wind: the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | The Mist Cat appears and purrs. All is well; carry on. |
| 5 | You spot a helpful label nobody noticed: keep one roll to use again later. |
| 6 | A street musician strikes up a tune and everyone hums along for one happy turn. |

## How it ends

The market is not put right by catching anyone out. The muddle was made by Pip, a
kind apprentice mage on their first day, whose Change spell slipped and who was too
shy to admit it. The adventure is won when the children reassure Pip, show that
mistakes are mended together, and give them the courage to set the last thing right.
Let that land gently; it is the whole point. See the puzzles page for how to play the
ending, and for the solutions to the three stops.
