# The Sleeping Garden: Rules for the Grown-Up

*New to this? Read the Guide for the Grown-Up first. It explains the whole idea in
two pages and you will never need it again.*

You are the Game Master, which on the Isles means a few small jobs at once:
narrator, gentle referee, the children's biggest fan, and a fellow adventurer who
plays along. This is a cooperative game for two or more who play together, a grown-up
and one or more children, all on the same side. Nobody competes, and nobody loses.

## Setting up

- Print the map. Each player puts a small figure (a building-brick minifigure
  works well) on the Start.
- Each player chooses one magic: Light, Grow, Change, or Voice. Two different
  magics work best, because combining them is half the fun.
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). These are
  spent and refilled during play.
- Build the objects that come up (a key, a flower, a bridge) from household bits
  and bricks as you go. Anything can be anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## Difficulty bands, not dice

Challenges never call for a particular die. Instead each one has a band: **Easy**,
**Normal**, or **Hard**. When the players try something, look up the band on the
kit's dice-band table, which tells you the number to meet or beat with whatever
dice your family owns. Every challenge in this story can be played with a single
ordinary die, and richer dice sets simply make it feel grander.

## The golden rule: here nobody loses

A failed roll is never a defeat. It is a detour to a different route. When a roll
does not meet the band, the players always have a way through:

- **Spend an energy star** to try the same idea again, or
- **Combine two magics** once per stop to pass the challenge with no roll at
  all (Light and Voice, say, or Grow and Change, working as one).

Energy stars come back when the players move on to the next stop, and when they
help each other. So nobody can ever run dry for long, and nobody is ever stuck.

## Helping each other

At any moment one player may help another instead of acting alone. When they
do, make the help roll: on a success the challenge is passed for the pair, even
if the first roll missed. Helping is generous, so reward it; it also tops up an
energy star. The lesson underneath the whole game is that you get further
together.

## The surprise

At each stop, once, the players may roll for a surprise and you read out the
matching line. Use whichever die the dice-band table gives you for surprises; with
a single ordinary die, read the line that matches the number rolled.

| Roll | Surprise |
|---|---|
| 1 | A Messenger Butterfly flutters past and gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | You find a glimmer of energy: take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A kindly breeze: the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | The Mist Cat appears and purrs. All is well; carry on. |
| 5 | A wise creature lets you repeat one roll later, whenever you like. |
| 6 | A little song drifts by and everyone dances for one happy turn. |

## How it ends

The Heart of the Garden is not a battle. The "antagonist" is the Lonely Sprite,
who sent the garden to sleep only because it wanted a friend. The adventure is won
when the children offer it friendship. Let that land gently; it is the whole
point. See the puzzles page for how to play the heart, and for the solutions to
the three stops.
