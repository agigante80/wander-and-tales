# The Sleeping Garden

*The same adventure as the simple telling, with a little more room to breathe.
Read it aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. The beats and the
puzzles are identical; only the language has grown up.*

## Before you begin

This morning you wake to a silence so complete that it feels like a held breath.
From the high window of the House of the Little Mages you look down over the home
island, and your stomach tightens. The Great Garden, the green heart of the whole
island, has turned grey and motionless, as though something has coaxed it into a
deep sleep. The flowers are shut tight. The fountain, which usually murmurs all
day, makes no sound at all.

You are two young mages, and this is your first real adventure. You shoulder your
bag, glance at each other, and without quite planning to, you say the same words
at the same moment: "Let us go and wake it up." Below you, the golden path winds
away into the quiet.

*Choose your mage name and your magic, Light, Grow, Change, or Voice, before you
take the first step.*

## Stop 1: The Vine Gate

The path brings you to a tall gate, and your way is blocked. Over the seasons the
vines have grown across it and pulled themselves into an enormous, stubborn knot
of leaves, and the gate will not give so much as an inch.

Something stirs in the haze beside the path. A cat the colour of fog pads out and
sits, curling its tail neatly around its paws. It is the Mist Cat, and everyone
on the island knows it turns up exactly when a hint might be useful. It studies
the knot, then turns its calm eyes to you, as if to say: well, what shall we do
about this?

*How do you open the way? You might coax a gap to grow, change the knot into
something looser, or simply ask the vines, politely, to step aside. See what you
can invent.*

The moment the gate swings wide, a single sleepy flower unfurls one petal, as if
testing whether morning has truly come. The garden is waking, just a little.

## Stop 2: The Flower Bed

Beyond the gate lies a wide flower bed, and here something has gone gently wrong.
Four flowers have drooped and forgotten where they belong, their colours muddled.
As you draw near, a thread of a voice hums an old planting rhyme, the kind a
gardener might teach a child: "First the sun, then the sea, then the berry, then
fly free."

The flowers clearly want to stand in their proper order again. They have simply
forgotten which colour comes where, and they are hoping you can work it out.

*Each line of the rhyme is a clue to a colour. Which flower is the sun, which is
the sea? Set the four of them in the order the rhyme sings, and see what
happens.*

As the last flower slips into place, all four lift their heads and open wide,
turning their faces up to a sky that is slowly bluing. Two more corners of the
garden shake off their grey.

## Stop 3: The Talking Fountain

The path opens onto a wide stone fountain, and the first thing you notice is the
silence. No splash, no trickle, not even a drip. The water lies flat and grey as
an old coin, and somehow the whole fountain seems to droop, as if it has
forgotten how to be glad. When you lean in to listen, it lets out a long, low
sigh. It is not broken. It is only waiting for a reason to sing again.

*How do you coax it back? A song, a joke, a few kind words? The fountain does not
need fixing so much as cheering. What will you try?*

At the first sign of real cheer the water leaps upward, catches the light, and
breaks into a bright, splashing tune it had clearly been longing to sing. Now
only the very heart of the garden remains asleep.

## The Heart of the Garden

At the centre of the Great Garden, where the golden path finally ends, you find
the cause of all of it: a small sprite, curled up as tight as a fist, with a face
full of worry. This is the one who sent the garden to sleep. Not out of malice,
you realise, and not as any kind of villain. It was simply lonely, so lonely that
it did not know how to ask for company, and a sleeping garden was the only way it
knew to keep anyone near.

There is nothing here to defeat. There never was.

*What do you say to the sprite? How might you show it, in words or in a gesture,
that it does not have to be alone any more?*

When you offer your friendship, plainly and kindly, the sprite's worried face
softens into the first smile it has worn in a very long time. And all at once the
Great Garden wakes around you: petals spring open, the fountain sings, the Mist
Cat stretches in a patch of new sunlight, and warmth pours back across the whole
island.

You solved it the way every adventure on the Isles is solved, together, by being
clever and by being kind. Well done, both of you.
