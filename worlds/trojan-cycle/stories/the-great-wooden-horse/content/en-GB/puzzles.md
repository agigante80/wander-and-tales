# The Great Wooden Horse: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need
to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always
moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward imagination and kindness
over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: Building the Horse (Normal)

**The challenge.** The great wooden horse must be built tall, strong, and steady
on its wheels, with a little door in its side. The players help the carpenters
work out how the wooden pieces fit and balance, and how many planks the build will
need.

**Intended solution.** This is a moment of Imagination, Logic, and a little Spatial
thinking and Maths. The bright move is to use **Penelope's thread** to measure a
piece before it is cut, and to count the planks together so each leg matches and
the body balances. Sorting the pieces by shape, or laying out a simple plan before
building, works just as well. Settling on any sensible, balanced plan passes the
stop.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have a friendly carpenter hold up two
planks and ask, "Which of these is longer, do you think?", or let them spend an
energy star to "measure twice and cut once". Counting out loud together always
gets there. Any steady plan raises the horse and tops up an energy star.

## Stop 2: Filling It with Friendship (Easy)

**The challenge.** The horse is built and its door stands open. The players choose
what to put inside. The whole idea is a kind surprise, so the answer is never
soldiers and never anything frightening, only friendly things.

**Intended solution.** This is a gentle Social-emotional and Logic moment. Any
warm, welcoming choice works: the **basket of honey and figs** to share, garlands
of bright **flowers**, and a **little drum** for happy music. Ask the children
which gifts say "let us be friends", and let them explain why. Naming any kind
gift, and saying why it is kind, passes the stop.

**Fallback.** If a child suggests something fierce, gently wonder aloud together,
"How would the city folk feel if they opened the door and found that? What would
make them smile instead?" Steer warmly towards the figs, the flowers, and the
drum. Choosing any kind thing is a full success and tops up an energy star.

## Stop 3: The Surprise at the Gates (Normal)

**The challenge.** The heroes roll the horse up to the city gates, which stay shut.
The watchful folk on the walls look wary and weary. The players may feel this is a
standoff. It is not. It is a gift waiting to be opened.

**Intended solution.** This is a moment of Courage and Social-emotional warmth. The
city is not an enemy, only tired and unsure. Any genuine, friendly gesture works:
sounding the **conch horn** as a warm hello, opening the horse's door, sharing the
**figs**, calling up a kind word, or beating the little drum. When the surprise of
friendship lands, the great gates swing open. Showing the city any clear sign of
friendship passes the stop.

**Fallback.** If the children freeze, or imagine the gates will never open, have a
single curious child appear at the top of the wall and call down, "Is that... for
us?" The smallest kindness, a wave, a fig held up, a soft note on the horn, melts
the wariness and the gates begin to open.

## The ending

There is no battle and no loser, and there never was a soldier in the horse. When
the surprise of friendship works, the proud city opens its gates, the two camps
spread a feast together on the hillside, and **Thetis the Sea-Mother** smiles from
the calm water. The heroes have won the only way anyone wins here, by being brave,
loyal to a friend, and full of ideas.
