# The Great Wooden Horse

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only brave new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

For a long, long time the two camps on the windswept shores of Troy have been
cross with each other. The seafarers live in tents and wooden ships along the
beach. The proud city sits up on the hill behind great honey-coloured walls, with
its gates shut tight. Nobody quite remembers how the quarrel began. The truth is,
everyone is rather tired of it, and both camps secretly wish it would just stop.

Odysseus the Clever calls you to him. He has a curly beard and a kind, twinkly
smile, and in his hands he holds a tiny wooden horse. "I have a clever idea," he
says. "Let us build a great wooden horse, as big as a house. But we will not fill
it with soldiers. We will fill it with gifts and music and friendship, and roll
it to the gates as a happy surprise. That is how we end the quarrel."

He gives you three gifts: a basket of honey and figs, Penelope's thread, and a
conch horn. Then he smiles. "On these shores we do not win by being the
strongest. We win by being clever, brave, and loyal to a friend."

*What is your hero name? Will you carry Cleverness, Courage, or Loyalty?*

## Stop 1: Building the Horse

On the beach lie heaps of smooth wooden planks. The horse must be built tall and
strong, so it stands up without wobbling, with a little door in its side.

The builders scratch their heads. How many planks for each leg? How do you fit
the curved pieces so they balance? Penelope's thread can measure each piece, and
careful counting will tell you how many you need.

*How could you build it so it stands up straight? Could you measure with the
thread? Could you count the planks together? Try something and see.*

When you fit the pieces and balance them just right, the great wooden horse rises
up on its wheels, tall and proud and steady. Everyone cheers.

## Stop 2: Filling It with Friendship

Now the horse is built, and its little door stands open. What should you put
inside? Not soldiers, never soldiers. The whole idea is a kind surprise.

You look at all the lovely things on the beach: the basket of figs, garlands of
bright flowers, and a little drum for happy music.

*What kind things would make the best surprise? Which gifts say "let us be
friends"? Choose together. There is no wrong answer here.*

When you tuck the figs, the flowers, and the little drum inside, the horse feels
warm and welcoming, like a present waiting to be opened. It is the friendliest
horse anyone has ever seen.

## Stop 3: The Surprise at the Gates

Slowly, slowly, you roll the great horse up the hill to the city gates. The folk
on the walls peer down. They look wary and weary. The gates stay shut. For a
moment, your heart thumps.

But this is not a fight. It is a gift. You only need to show them it is kind.

*What could you do to show them you come as friends? You have the conch horn to
sound a warm hello. You could open the horse's door and share the figs. What do
you try?*

When you sound the conch horn and open the door, out tumble figs and flowers and
a merry drumbeat. The watchful folk gasp, then laugh, then beam. Slowly, the
great gates creak open at last.

## The Long Quarrel Ends

The city folk come down from their walls, and the seafarers come up from the
beach, and they meet in the middle, a little shy. Then someone shares a fig.
Someone beats the little drum. Someone laughs, and then everyone laughs.

The two camps spread out a great feast together, side by side, as if the quarrel
had never been. Out on the calm water, Thetis the Sea-Mother lifts her head from
the waves and smiles. You did it together. You were clever, you were brave, and
you were loyal, and the oldest quarrel of all has ended with a kind surprise.
Well done, heroes of Troy.
