# The Long Way Home

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only brave new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

The long quarrel is over, and it ended not with spears but with friendship. The
great gates of Troy stand open, and now it is time to sail home across the
Wine-Dark Sea. Far away waits the Weaving Hall, where a warm fire, a homecoming
feast, and old friends are ready for you.

Odysseus the Bright walks you down to the ships on the long sandy beach. "It is a
long way home," he says, "and the sea is wide. But you are full of ideas, you are
brave, and you keep faith with your friends. That is all a sailor truly needs."

He gives you three gifts for the journey: a great spiral Conch Horn, a Basket of
Honey and Figs, and a ball of strong thread, Penelope's Thread. Then he smiles.
"Out there we do not win by being the strongest. We win by being brave, loyal,
and full of ideas."

*What is your hero name? Are you a hero of Imagination, of Courage, or of Loyalty?*

## Stop 1: The Isle of Winds

You sail for many days until you come to a floating island ringed with shining
bronze cliffs. This is the Isle of Winds, where Aeolus, Keeper of the Winds,
lives. He keeps all the winds of the world tied up safe in a great leather bag.

Aeolus is kind, but he is careful. He will only lend a fair wind to travellers
who ask with a good idea and politely, and who are not greedy. "Ask me nicely," he says,
"and never, ever peek inside the wind-bag."

*How will you ask for a fair wind? Could you think of good, kind words to say?
Could you promise not to peek inside the bag? What do you try?*

When you ask with a good idea and kindly, Aeolus laughs and ties a gentle wind into your
sail. "A fair wind for friends," he says, and waves you off across the water.

## Stop 2: The Singing Sirens

For a long while you sail on. Then you hear it: a song floating over the sea,
beautiful and so very sad. It comes from the Singing Sirens, sea-singers who sit
on the lonely rocks. Their song is so lonely that ships drift off course just to
follow it.

This is not a danger to run from. Listen closely. The Sirens are not wicked at
all. They are only lonely, because nobody ever stays to be their friend.

*This is not a fight, and it is not a race to escape. What kind thing could you
do? You could sing a song back to them. You could sound the Conch Horn. You could
share your Basket of Honey and Figs. What do you try?*

When you answer their loneliness with kindness, the Sirens stop their sad song.
They laugh, they wave, and they begin to sing a bright and happy tune that guides
your ship safely past the rocks. "Come back and visit us!" they call.

## Stop 3: Home at Last

At last, far ahead, you see a shore you know. Your heart beats fast. Is this
really home? You must look closely and be sure.

You step onto the sand and find the path up to the Weaving Hall. There, through
the door, you can see the great loom where a friend has been weaving while you
were away. An old friend looks up. And there, by the fire, an old dog lifts his
grey head and sniffs the air.

*How will you know you are truly home? What do you remember about this place?
Look for the loom, for an old friend, for the faithful hound. Who waits by the
fire?*

It is Argos the Faithful Hound! He has never forgotten you. He trots up, his tail
wagging so hard it nearly knocks him over, and you are home at last.

## Home at Last

The Weaving Hall is warm and full of light. The homecoming feast is laid out: warm
bread, sweet figs, and honey. Old friends hug you and call your hero name. Argos
the Faithful Hound will not leave your side.

High above the calm water, Thetis the Sea-Mother smiles down on you. You crossed
the whole Wine-Dark Sea and came home together. You were brave, you were full of ideas,
and most of all you kept faith with your friends. Well done, heroes. Welcome home.
