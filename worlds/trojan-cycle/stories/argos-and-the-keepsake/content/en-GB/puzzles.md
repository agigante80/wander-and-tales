# Argos and the Lost Keepsake: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need
to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always
moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward cleverness and loyalty
over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: Following Argos (Easy)

**The challenge.** Argos has the scent, but the players must read the tracks and
clues along the shore to choose which way their friend walked. Some marks lead
onward; some lead nowhere.

**Intended solution.** This is a moment of Observation and Loyalty. Have the
children look closely with Argos and sort the clues: the footprints that carry on in
one clear direction, the dropped fig and the resting scuff that sit along that same
line, against any stray marks that double back or peter out. Following the clues that
agree with one another, and trusting Argos when he barks happily, sends them the
right way. Any child who slows down, looks carefully, and stays on the trail has
solved it.

**Fallback.** If they are unsure, let Argos take a few steps the right way and look
back, or let them spend an energy star to "look again" and notice that the deep,
onward footprints all point the same way. Sticking close to the dog and not giving
up always finds the trail; this stop is meant to feel like an easy, happy start.

## Stop 2: The Tide Pools (Normal)

**The challenge.** The trail leads in among three sunlit tide pools, and the players
must work out which pool their friend rested beside, where a thread slipped from his
cloak.

**The worked example.** Read the scene so the three pools are clear. Beside **pool
one** lies a white shell. Beside **pool two** lies a striped shell. Beside **pool
three** lies a smooth grey pebble. Argos gives two clues to hold in mind at once: the
scent **passed a striped shell** (so not the white-shell pool), and the scent **did
not stop where the pebble was** (so not the pebble pool). Cross off pool one and pool
three, and only **pool two**, the striped-shell pool with no pebble, is left. That is
where the dropped thread is found, pointing the way on.

**Intended solution.** This is a Memory, Logic and Observation moment. The clever
move is to remember both of Argos's clues and use them to rule pools out one at a
time until a single pool remains. Counting on fingers, pointing at each pool in turn,
or laying a token by each one as they reason are all good.

**Fallback.** If the children lose the thread of it, simplify to one clue at a time:
"Argos says not the white shell, so which pools are left?" then "Argos says not the
pebble, so which one is it?" Or let them spend an energy star while Argos sniffs
straight to the right pool. Reaching pool two by any route counts as a full success
and tops up an energy star.

## Stop 3: The Far Dune (Normal)

**The challenge.** The keepsake has fallen near a lonely dune and the wind has half
buried it in the sand. Argos finds the spot; the players must dig it out gently and
reunite their friend with it.

**Intended solution.** This is an Observation and Loyalty moment. The clever, kind
move is to watch where Argos points his nose, kneel at that exact spot, and dig
carefully together, brushing the sand away a little at a time and watching for a
corner of olive wood rather than scrabbling and scattering it. When they do, the
carved token comes up warm and whole, and Argos frees the last of it with one gentle
paw. Patience and care, not force, win the day.

**Fallback.** If a child digs in the wrong place or grows frustrated, have Argos pad
over, sniff once, and start the digging himself right where it should be, so they can
join in. Spending an energy star to "look closer" also reveals the glint of wood. The
keepsake is always found; the only question is how tenderly.

## The ending

There is no battle and no loser. When the keepsake is carried home, Lykos is
overjoyed and hugs both the token and Argos, Odysseus the Clever praises the heroes
for leaving no one behind, and **Thetis the Sea-Mother** smiles on the calm sea so
the ships may sail home with every friend and treasure safe aboard. The heroes have
won the only way anyone wins here, by being clever, brave, and loyal.
