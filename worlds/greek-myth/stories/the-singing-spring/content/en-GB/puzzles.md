# The Singing Spring: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need
to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always
moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward cleverness and kindness
over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Riddle Gate (Normal)

**The challenge.** The Riddle Sphinx will not open the gate until the players
answer her riddle. She is fair and patient, and she enjoys a good thinking face.

**The riddle.** Read it aloud: *"I have roots that nobody sees. I am taller than the
tallest trees. Up, up, up I go, and yet I never grow. What am I?"* The answer is **a
mountain** (think of Olympus rising above you).

**Intended solution.** This is a thinking challenge of Wits and a little Vocabulary.
Let the children talk it through together. The sphinx accepts the right answer, and
because she is kind, she also accepts a brave, thoughtful guess.

**Fallback.** If they are stuck, the sphinx offers a clue ("You can see me from your
town, and the gods live on my head"), or Bramble the satyr whispers the answer from
behind a rock. A guess made bravely still opens the gate.

## Stop 2: The Winding Maze (Normal)

**The challenge.** Beyond the gate is a maze of stone walls where every path looks
the same. The players must find their way through without getting lost.

**Intended solution.** This is a Spatial and Logic moment. The clever move is to use
the **golden thread**: tie one end at the entrance and unspool it as you go, so you
can always retrace your steps and never walk the same wrong path twice. Marking each
turn, or asking **Bramble the Satyr** for a hint, works just as well.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have Bramble pop up and point the way with
a joke, or let them spend an energy star to "remember" the route. Getting through
with help still counts as a full success and tops up an energy star.

## Stop 3: The Lonely Cyclops (Hard, then no roll)

**The challenge.** A huge one-eyed giant sits in front of the spring with his arms
folded. He looks fearsome, and the players may feel this is a fight. It is not.

**Intended solution.** This is a Heart and Courage moment. The giant is simply
lonely; nobody ever visits him. Any genuine kindness works: sharing the **honey
cake**, asking his name, telling him he is not alone, inviting him to be a friend.
When the children are kind, he moves aside with happy tears. Then they wake the
spring by playing the **little lyre**, and the **Singing Nymphs** rise and sing.

**Fallback.** If the children freeze, or want to "win" by force, have the giant
sniffle and ask, very quietly, "Are you scared of me too?" A single kind word melts
him. The moment the players try any tune at all, the nymphs wake and the water
sings.

## The ending

There is no battle and no loser. When the spring sings, every well in the Olive
Town fills again, the giant has friends at last, and wise **Athena** smiles down
from the mountain. The heroes have won the only way anyone wins here, by being
clever, brave, and kind.
