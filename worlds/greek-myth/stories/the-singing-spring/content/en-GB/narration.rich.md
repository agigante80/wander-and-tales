# The Singing Spring

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause at each question so
the heroes can decide together. There are no wrong answers here, only braver ideas
to try.*

## Before you begin

For as long as anyone could remember, the Singing Spring had sung high in the
hills, and its song had kept every well in the Olive Town cool and brimming. Then,
one grey morning, the song simply stopped. The wells sank low. The olive leaves
hung limp and thirsty, and the whole valley felt as if it were holding its breath.

Old Daphne, keeper of the olive grove, called the two of you to her side. "You have
bright ideas and kind hearts," she said, "and that is worth more up there than any
sword. Will you climb the hill and wake the spring?"

Into your hands she pressed three gifts: a ball of golden thread, a honey cake
wrapped in a leaf, and a small lyre worn smooth with playing. "Remember," she said,
"in these hills we do not win by being the strongest. We win by being brave,
kind, and full of ideas."

*What is your hero name? Which quality will you carry: Imagination, Courage, or Heart?*

## Stop 1: The Riddle Gate

The road climbed into the sun, and at the top stood a tall gate of white marble. On
its warm steps lay a sphinx, her body a lion's, her wings folded gold, her face
calm and curious. She watched you with the patience of someone who has all the time
in the world.

"None may pass," she said, "until they answer my riddle." There was no cruelty in
it. If anything, she looked pleased to have visitors who might actually think.

*Will you listen closely and puzzle it out together? A brave, thoughtful guess is
welcome too. What do you try?*

When your answer came, the sphinx dipped her great wings and drew the gate aside.
"Well thought," she said, almost smiling, and let you through.

## Stop 2: The Winding Maze

Beyond the gate the path split, and split again, into a maze of old stone walls
where every turning looked exactly like the last. One wrong choice and you might
wander until dark.

A goat-legged satyr swung up onto a wall, grinning. It was Bramble, who loved a
traveller and a good joke in equal measure. He hummed a tune and tipped his head
down one shadowed lane.

*How will you keep from losing your way? You carry a ball of golden thread, and
Bramble seems willing to help. What is your plan?*

By tying the thread at the entrance and letting it trail behind you, every step
became a step you could undo. The maze could not trap a hero with a thread, and
soon you stepped out into the long golden light of evening.

## Stop 3: The Lonely Giant

And there, at last, was the spring. But a giant sat squarely in front of it: one
enormous eye, two folded arms, and a face set hard as the cliff behind him. Your
heart thumped. He looked, for a moment, like the end of the adventure.

Then he sniffed. And you understood. He was not angry. He was lonely. No traveller
had ever stayed to speak with him, and the long silence had made him gruff.

*This was never a fight. What kindness could you show him? You have a honey cake to
share. You could ask his name, or simply tell him that he is not alone. What do you
do?*

When you offered your kindness, the giant's huge eye brimmed over, and he wiped it
with the back of one enormous hand. "You stayed," he whispered, as though he could
hardly believe it. "You are my friends." And he shuffled, very gently, aside.

## The Spring Sings Again

You stood at the edge of the quiet water and lifted the little lyre. The tune you
played was simple and a touch wobbly, but it came straight from the heart, and that
was all the spring had been waiting for.

The surface trembled. Up through the spray rose the Singing Nymphs, laughing, their
voices weaving into a song so bright it seemed to lift the whole hillside. The
spring leapt back to life, and far below, every well in the Olive Town brimmed cool
and clear once more.

High on cloud-wrapped Olympus, kind Athena looked down and smiled. You had done it,
and you had done it together: with your ideas, with your courage, and above all with
your kindness. Well done, heroes of the Sunlit Hills.
