# Athena's Little Owl: Rules for the Grown-Up

*New to this? Read the Guide for the Grown-Up first. It explains the whole idea in
two pages and you will never need it again.*

The Guide for the Grown-Up explains how the game works: this page holds only what is
special about this story. It is a gentle, kindly tale, and no one in it is ever truly
hurt or frightened for long. A failed roll is never a defeat; nobody loses.

## Setting up

- Print the map. Each player puts a small figure on the Start, the Olive Town.
- This world has no magic. Instead each hero leans on one of three qualities:
  **Imagination** (dreaming up ideas), **Courage** (a steady heart), or **Heart** (caring for
  others). On the hero sheet, where it says magic, let each child write the quality
  they like best. Two different qualities work best together.
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). They are spent
  and refilled during play.
- Set out a prop or two if you can: anything small and soft to stand in for Glaux the
  owl, and a feather or a scrap of paper or two for her trail. Anything can be
  anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## The Twist of Fate

Once at each place the players may roll for a twist; read the matching line. The Guide
explains how it works.

| Roll | Twist of Fate |
|---|---|
| 1 | A friendly satyr gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | You find your courage again, and take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A favourable wind, and the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | Chiron the centaur passes by and nods kindly. All is well. |
| 5 | An olive branch for luck: keep one roll to use again later. |
| 6 | A burst of birdsong, and for a moment everyone feels brave. |

## How it ends

There is no foe in this tale at all, only a small lost owl and the kindness it takes
to bring her home. The adventure is won by helping Glaux feel safe, choosing the true
path with sharp eyes, and giving the tired little owl a gentle hand up to her perch.
Let the children lead with care; that is the whole point. See the puzzles page for the
three challenges and their solutions.
