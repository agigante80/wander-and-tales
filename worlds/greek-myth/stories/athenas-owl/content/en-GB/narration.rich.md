# Athena's Little Owl

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause at each question so the
heroes can decide together. There are no wrong answers here, only kinder ideas to
try.*

## Before you begin

The fog came down over the hills before dawn, soft and grey and thick as wool, and
with it came a small, lost sound: the frightened hoot of an owl. When the Olive Town
woke, there in the middle of the square sat a tiny owl, her feathers ruffled and her
round eyes wide. Her name was Glaux, and she was the companion of wise Athena, whose
temple stood high on the hill of white marble. In the fog she had lost her way, and
she could not find the road home.

Old Daphne, keeper of the olive grove, called the two of you to her side. "You have
sharp eyes and kind hearts," she said, "and that is worth more this morning than the
fastest pair of legs in Greece. Will you guide little Glaux safely back up to
Athena?"

Gently she wrapped the owl in a soft scarf and placed her in your care. "Remember,"
she said, "up here we do not help by being the biggest or the boldest. We help by
being clever, brave, and kind."

*What is your hero name? Which quality will you carry: Wits, Courage, or Heart?*

## Stop 1: The Foggy Square

Glaux huddled in the heart of the square, so small that the cobblestones seemed huge
around her. Her feathers stood on end, her round eyes blinked and blinked, and each
time a foot scuffed too near she startled and hopped away. She was far too
frightened to be carried anywhere yet.

*How could you help her feel safe before you set out? Might you move slowly and keep
your voices soft? Might you hold out a steady hand and simply wait for her to choose
you? What do you try?*

When you grew calm and gentle and let her come to you in her own time, Glaux stopped
her trembling. She studied you with those wise round eyes, gave a small contented
hoot, and hopped up onto your waiting hand. From that moment she trusted you
completely.

## Stop 2: The Whispering Paths

Where the town ended, three narrow paths wound up into the mist, and in the grey
half-light each looked exactly like the others. Only one of them climbed to Athena's
temple; the other two would lead you in circles until the day was gone.

A goat-legged satyr swung himself up onto a wall, grinning through the fog. It was
Bramble, who loved a traveller and a good joke in equal measure, and who was always
glad to point the way for heroes he liked.

*How will you choose the right path? Look closely at the ground. As Glaux tumbled
down in the night she shed soft grey feathers along the way. Could you follow that
trail back up? Could you ask cheerful Bramble for a hint? What is your plan?*

By following the scattered feathers, or by asking kind Bramble for his help, you
found the one true path, and you began to climb together through the drifting mist.

## Stop 3: The High Perch

At the summit stood a small temple of white marble, its columns rising into the last
of the fog. High among them was Glaux's perch, a snug hollow where she slept each
night. But the little owl was weary now, and frightened, and the mist still hid her
roost. She did not dare make the flight alone.

*This was never about being strong. How might you help her up? Could you lift a
steady branch to make a bridge for her small feet? Could you cheer her on, so that
she found her courage? What do you do?*

When you helped her with patience and kindness, Glaux gathered herself, spread her
small wings, and fluttered up to her perch at last. There she settled into the warm
hollow, safe and snug, and tucked her head beneath one wing.

## Glaux Comes Home

As the owl nestled down, two calm grey eyes opened within the temple, bright as
polished silver. It was Athena herself, goddess of wisdom and good ideas, and she was
smiling down at the two of you.

At her smile the morning fog began to lift, thinning and curling and floating away,
until the sun broke through and poured golden light across the Olive Town far below.
From her high perch Glaux gave one clear, happy hoot, as if to say thank you.

You had done it, and you had done it together: with your wits, with your courage, and
above all with your kindness. Well done, heroes of the Sunlit Hills.
