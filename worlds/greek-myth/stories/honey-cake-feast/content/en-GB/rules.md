# The Honey-Cake Feast: Rules for the Grown-Up

*New to this? Read the Guide for the Grown-Up first. It explains the whole idea in two
pages and you will never need it again.*

The Guide for the Grown-Up explains how the game works: this page holds only what is
special about this story. It is a warm, gentle tale about sharing and making room for
everyone, and no one in it is ever truly hurt or left out. A failed roll is never a
defeat; nobody loses.

## Setting up

- Print the map. Each player puts a small figure on the Start, the Olive Town.
- This world has no magic. Instead each hero leans on one of three qualities:
  **Imagination** (dreaming up ideas), **Courage** (a steady heart), or **Heart** (caring for
  others). On the hero sheet, where it says magic, let each child write the quality
  they like best. Two different qualities work best together.
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). They are spent and
  refilled during play.
- Set out a prop or two if you can: an empty jar or cup for the honey, a few buttons or
  beads for flowers, and a round biscuit or a paper circle for the honey cake. Anything
  can be anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## The Twist of Fate

Once at each place the players may roll for a twist; read the matching line. The Guide
explains how it works.

| Roll | Twist of Fate |
|---|---|
| 1 | A friendly satyr gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | You find your courage again, and take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A favourable wind, and the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | Chiron the centaur passes by and nods kindly. All is well. |
| 5 | An olive branch for luck: keep one roll to use again later. |
| 6 | A burst of birdsong, and for a moment everyone feels brave. |

## How it ends

There is no foe in this tale. The Cyclops was once the lonely giant of the spring, but
he is a friend now, and the only challenge left is a happy one: to make him feel truly
welcome. The adventure is won by asking the bees kindly, sharing the cake so that
everyone has enough, and giving a shy friend the courage to come to the table. Let the
sharing and the welcome land; that is the whole point. See the puzzles page for the
three challenges and their solutions.
