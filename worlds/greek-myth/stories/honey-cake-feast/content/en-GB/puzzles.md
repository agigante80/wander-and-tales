# The Honey-Cake Feast: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need to
work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always moves
forward. More than one good idea should work; reward fresh ideas and kindness over the
"right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Hill of Bees (Easy)

**The challenge.** The Hill Bees keep the sweetest honey, but they will only share
with someone who comes gently. The players need to fill the empty jar.

**Intended solution.** This is a Heart and Observation moment. The kind move is to
slow down and stay calm, not to swat or snatch: approach softly, offer the bees a few
meadow flowers, and ask politely. Happy bees fill the jar themselves.

**Fallback.** If the children rush and the bees scatter, have **Bramble the Satyr**
show them how to hum a low, friendly note, or let a player spend an energy star to
calm everyone down. Asking kindly always works, and tops up an energy star.

## Stop 2: Milo's Kitchen (Normal)

**The challenge.** Milo's honey cake must be shared so that every guest at the feast
gets a piece and nobody is left out, including the very large Cyclops.

**Intended solution.** This is a Maths and Heart moment. Count the guests together (a
handful of townsfolk, Daphne, Milo, the two heroes, and the giant). Cut a slice for
each, and remember that fair does not mean identical: the giant is ten times the size,
so he needs a much bigger slice, while the smallest child is happy with a small one.
Any plan where everyone has enough is a success.

**Fallback.** If the counting feels hard, have Daphne count the guests aloud on her
fingers, or let the children line up one biscuit or pebble per guest and match them to
the slices. Getting there with help still counts as a full success and tops up an
energy star.

## Stop 3: Fetching the Friend (Normal)

**The challenge.** The Cyclops is shy to come down to a whole townful of people. He
fears he is too big, or that the townsfolk will be afraid of him. The players must give
him the courage to come.

**Intended solution.** This is a Heart and Courage moment. Any honest, warm
reassurance works: telling him plainly that he is wanted, reminding him that the town
are his friends now, showing him the grand slice saved just for him, or offering to
walk down at his side. Kindness, not any plan, carries this stop.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure what to say, have the giant ask quietly, "Are
you really sure there is room for me?" A single kind word is enough, and down the hill
he comes.

## The ending

There is no battle and no loser. When the honey is gathered, the cake is shared, and
the giant takes his seat, the whole **Olive Town** feasts together under the olive
trees with nobody left out, and kind **Athena** smiles down from the mountain. The
heroes have won the only way anyone wins here, by being brave, kind, and full of ideas.
