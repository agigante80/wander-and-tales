# The Moura's Lost Comb: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players
need to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child
always moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward good ideas and
kindness over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Quiet Spring (Easy)

**The challenge.** The Moura Encantada has lost her golden comb and is too shy to
say much. She will only point the way once the players have earned her trust with
calm, gentle kindness.

**Intended solutions.** This is a social, gentle-heart moment more than a magic
trick:
- **Riversong** is the natural fit: speak softly to the spring, and let the water
  carry a kind word to the Moura.
- **Bloom** can soften the mood, coaxing a few flowers up at the water's edge as a
  small gift.
- A simple kind action with no magic at all (sitting quietly, speaking gently,
  promising to help) works just as well.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure what to say, have the Moura ask a tiny
question of her own ("Have you come from the village? Is the fountain very sad
without its water?") to give them a thread to pull. Any genuine kindness opens her
up and tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, just have everyone say one kind thing to
the Moura, or wave hello gently, and let that be enough for her to smile and point
to the trail.

## Stop 2: The Stepping Stones (Normal)

**The challenge.** A line of carved stepping stones crosses a stream. The marks
run leaf, star, wave, leaf, star, and then a crumbled stone. The players must work
out which mark belongs on the missing stone and step on that one.

**Solution.** The pattern repeats in threes: **leaf, star, wave, leaf, star,
wave.** So the missing mark is the **wave**, and the wave stone is the safe step.
Logic and observation do the work here, not a magic, though **Riversong** can ask
the stream to confirm, and **Starlight** can light the carvings if the day is dim.

**Fallback.** If the children are stuck, read the watery rhyme again slowly,
tapping each stone in turn so they hear the three-beat pattern. Working it out with
help still counts as a full success and tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, lay out just three objects (leaf, star,
wave) and then leaf, star again, and ask only "what comes next?"

## Stop 3: The Trasgo's Nook (Normal)

**The challenge.** The Trasgo has the golden comb and loves it because it is
shiny. He will not give it up to scolding or grabbing. He is playful and lonely,
and he wants a game, a riddle, or a fair swap.

**Intended solutions.** This is a befriending moment:
- **A fair swap.** Offer him something he would love as much: a shiny carried
  object, a chip of mirror, a slice of rosca bread (he cannot resist its smell).
- **A riddle or a game.** Trade riddles, or play a quick game; win it, or simply
  play along generously, and he hands the comb over for the fun of it.
- **Truth** can gently show him the comb belongs to the Moura and the village
  needs it, and that a friend is better than a treasure. **Bloom** can brighten
  his lonely nook so he feels less alone. **Birdsong** can fill that nook with
  birds and song so the lonely Trasgo creeps out to listen and join in.

**Fallback.** If the children freeze, have the Trasgo propose the game himself ("I
will trade it for a good riddle. Got one?") so there is an obvious door to walk
through. The moment they make any friendly offer, let him laugh and give up the
comb.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, let a single shiny swap or one shared
joke be enough; the Trasgo is easily delighted.

## Stop 4: The Comb Returned (Easy)

**The challenge.** The players carry the comb back and return it to the Moura
Encantada. The only "test" is to do it gently and kindly, so the moment stays
warm.

**Intended solution.** Have the players hand the comb over with care and say
something kind. There is no roll to fail here. **Riversong** can let them tell the
spring the good news first; any gentle gesture completes it.

**Fallback.** If a child is shy, the Moura simply reaches out and takes the comb
with thanks. The success is guaranteed; this stop is about the warm feeling, not a
puzzle.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, just placing the comb in the Moura's
hands and watching the water light up is the whole of it.

## The Spring Sings Again (no roll)

This is the resolution, not a puzzle, so there is no band and no roll. The Moura
draws the comb through her hair, the spring wakes, and down in the square the Old
Fountain runs clear and sings again. Let the children feel the whole village's
relief and the Moura's cool, sweet blessing. They won by being gentle with a shy
spirit and making a friend of the Trasgo instead of an enemy, which is the only
way anyone wins among the Enchanted Springs.
