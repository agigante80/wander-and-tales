# The Moura's Lost Comb

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause often so everyone can
puzzle, wonder, and try ideas. There are no wrong answers here, only new paths to
follow.*

## Before you begin

The morning you remember best began with worried voices in the village square. The
Old Fountain, the mossy stone fountain that has run clear and sweet for as long as
anyone can recall, had gone silent overnight. Its water sat grey and still, and a
line of villagers stood about it with empty jugs, not knowing what to do.

An old woman in a shawl turned to you. "The fountain is fed by the Enchanted
Spring, high in the hills," she said, "and the water only runs sweet while all is
well up there. Something has gone wrong. You have brave hearts and quick minds.
Will you climb up and find out?"

You shouldered your bag, glanced at one another, and set off. The path wound up
out of the square and into the cool green hills, and the further you climbed the
quieter the world became.

*What is your hero name? Which one or two magics will you carry: Bloom, Truth,
Starlight, Riversong, or Birdsong?*

## Stop 1: The Quiet Spring

Near the top of the hills you found the spring: a clear pool ringed with mossy
stones and ferns, the very heart of all the village's water. Beside it sat a
serene maiden with long dark hair, her chin resting on her knees. This was the
Moura Encantada, the gentle spirit of the spring, and she looked close to tears.

"My golden comb is gone," she told you, so softly you had to lean in. "It is the
comb I draw through my hair to wake the water and keep it singing. Without it the
spring sleeps, and your fountain down in the square stays quiet and grey. I would
look for it myself, but I am too shy to leave the water, and too shy to face
whoever took it."

She is wary of loud voices and grabbing hands. She will open up only to a calm,
patient kindness.

*How do you set the Moura at ease so she will trust you? Could Riversong let you
speak softly to the spring on her behalf? What gentle words or small kindness
might coax her story out?*

When at last she trusted you, the Moura lifted her head and almost smiled. "There
is a trail," she said, pointing to a faint glint among the ferns, "a line of
shiny things, leading off down the hill. Follow it, and whatever you find at the
end of it, please be gentle. Not everything that takes a shiny thing means any
harm."

## Stop 2: The Stepping Stones

The shimmering trail led you to a shallow stream, too wide to jump. The only way
across was a winding line of old stepping stones, each one carved with a small
worn symbol. You read them aloud as you went: a leaf, a star, a wave, then a leaf
again, a star again, and then a stone whose mark had crumbled away, with deeper
water on either side.

From under the surface a small watery voice hummed the same little tune over and
over: "Leaf, star, wave, leaf, star, and then?"

*Look at the pattern the carvings make. Which symbol must the crumbled stone be?
Work out the next mark in the sequence, and that is the safe stone to step on.*

The moment you chose the wave stone, the stream stilled and let you pass dry-shod.
On the far bank the shiny trail gathered itself and ran on, straight under the
mossy arch of a little stone bridge.

## Stop 3: The Trasgo's Nook

Beneath the bridge you found a snug, mossy nook, and it glittered. Every ledge was
crowded with shiny odds and ends: a brass button, a chip of mirror, a curl of
ribbon. In the middle of it all sat a small goblin in a bright red cap, the
Trasgo, turning the golden comb over and over in his hands and humming to himself.

He grinned when he saw you, but his fingers tightened on the comb. "I found it
fair and square. It is the shiniest thing I ever found, and it is mine now." He
was not wicked, only playful and proud, and, you slowly realised, rather lonely
under his quiet bridge. What he loved most in the world was a game, a riddle, or a
fair swap.

*How do you win the Trasgo over? He will not be scolded or grabbed, but a clever
trade, a riddle, or a game he can enjoy might change his mind. What would delight
a playful, lonely goblin enough to part with his treasure?*

When you offered him something he loved just as much, or beat him at his own
riddle, the Trasgo threw back his head and laughed and pressed the comb into your
hands. "A good game," he admitted. "Come back and play sometime. It gets quiet,
down here under the bridge, with nobody to swap with."

## Stop 4: The Comb Returned

You carried the golden comb back up the hill, cradling it like a fledgling. The
Moura Encantada was waiting at the water's edge, and her eyes went wide and bright
when she saw what you held. You knelt and, very gently, laid the comb across her
open hands.

She drew it once through her long dark hair. At the very first stroke the spring
woke: the water lit clear and bright and rose in a slow, glowing arc that hung in
the air like a held breath, then fell back singing.

*How do you return the comb so the moment stays kind and calm? What do you say to
the Moura now that her spring is hers again?*

"Thank you," she said, and now she truly smiled. "You were gentle when you might
have been rough, and you made a friend of the Trasgo when you might have made an
enemy. That is why the water trusts you. Now go, and listen for it."

## The Spring Sings Again

You ran back down the winding path, and you heard the square before you saw it: a
bright, bubbling, splashing sound, and under it something almost like a song. The
Old Fountain was clear and full again, water dancing up into the sunlight while
children laughed and dipped their jugs and the whole village crowded round.

Cool and sweet, the Moura's blessing came down with the water, the way it had for
a hundred years and would for a hundred more.

You did it together. You were kind when it was easier to be quick, and you were
full of ideas when one idea was not enough. Well done.
