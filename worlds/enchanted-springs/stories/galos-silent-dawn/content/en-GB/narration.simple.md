# The Galo's Silent Dawn

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

It is very early in Barcelos, and something is wrong. The sky is grey. The market
should be waking up, but everyone is upset. The bright rooster, the Galo de
Barcelos, sits on his wall with his beak shut. He has lost his crow, so the
morning will not begin.

Worse still, the shiny silver cup from the market has gone missing, and the
townsfolk are blaming a tired traveller, just because he is a stranger. He looks
very sad. You know he would never steal.

You pick up your bag. "Let us help," you say. "Let us find the truth."

*What is your hero name? Which magics do you choose: Bloom, Truth, Starlight, or
Riversong?*

## Stop 1: The Silent Roost

You climb up to the Galo de Barcelos. Up close, he looks poorly and droopy. He
opens his beak, but no sound comes out. He is sad because he cannot crow the
truth and clear the traveller's name.

He needs cheering and a little kindness before his voice will come back.

*How do you help the Galo feel better? Could you speak gently to him, sing with
him, or fetch him a cool drink of spring water? What do you try?*

When the Galo feels cared for, he gives a tiny, scratchy cluck. His voice is
coming back, slowly. "Find the cup," he whispers, "and I will crow the truth."

## Stop 2: The Barcelos Market

You go down to the market stalls. On one stall is an empty cushion, where the
silver cup used to sit. There are no muddy footprints, no broken locks. Nobody
came in or out.

You look around for clues. High above, something glints near the old bell tower.
A few shiny ribbons and buttons are missing from the stalls too.

*If no person took the cup, who might have? What likes shiny things and can reach
a high place? Where does the glinting lead?*

When you follow the clues, they all point up, up to the top of the old bell
tower. Off you go.

## Stop 3: The Old Bell Tower

You climb the bell tower stairs and come out at the top, among the bells. There,
in a nest of buttons and ribbons, sits a little jackdaw with a grey cap. Tucked
in her nest, shining, is the silver cup.

She is not a thief. She just loves shiny things, and she is shy. She tilts her
head at you, holding the cup close.

*How do you make friends with the shy jackdaw? Could you offer her something else
that shines, or speak softly so she is not scared? What might she like?*

When you are gentle and offer a fair trade, the jackdaw hops aside and lets you
lift the silver cup from her nest. She even chirps a thank-you.

## Stop 4: The Cup Returned

You carry the silver cup back down to the market. You hold it up high for everyone
to see. "Here it is," you say. "Nobody stole it. A little jackdaw borrowed it for
her nest."

The townsfolk go quiet. They look at the tired traveller they had blamed. They
feel sorry for being unfair to a stranger.

*How do you help the townsfolk be kind to the traveller again? What could you say
so everyone feels better?*

When the traveller is welcomed and given some bread and a smile, the whole square
warms up. Now there is only one thing left to do.

## The Dawn Crow

You set the silver cup back on its cushion. The Galo de Barcelos puffs up his
chest. He opens his beak and, this time, out comes a big, bright crow that rings
across the rooftops.

The sun leaps up, golden and warm. The market wakes, the stalls open, and the
traveller waves goodbye with a happy heart. The Galo only ever crows the truth,
and today the truth was kind.

You did it together. You were kind, and you were full of ideas. Well done.
