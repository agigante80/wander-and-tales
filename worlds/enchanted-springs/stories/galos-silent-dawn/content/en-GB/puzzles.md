# The Galo's Silent Dawn: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players
need to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child
always moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward good ideas and
kindness over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Silent Roost (Easy)

**The challenge.** The Galo de Barcelos has lost his crow and his spirits. He will
not find his voice until the players cheer and care for him.

**Intended solutions.** A gentle-heart moment more than a magic trick:
- **Riversong** to fetch him a sip of cool spring water and ask the water to soothe
  his throat.
- **Bloom** to bring a little colour and life back to his drooping perch.
- **Birdsong** to call to him as one bird to another and coax his own song back out.
- A plain kind act with no magic: sitting with him, singing softly, or promising
  out loud to clear the traveller's name.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have the Galo manage a single weak cluck
in answer to any kindness, showing them it is working. Any genuine care brings his
voice partway back and tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, just stroke the rooster's feathers or
say one kind thing, and let that be enough for him to whisper the next clue.

## Stop 2: The Barcelos Market (Normal)

**The challenge.** The silver cup is gone with no thief in sight: no footprints, no
broken lock. The players must work out that no person took it, and follow the
trail of small shiny thefts (a ribbon, a button, a glint by the tower) upward to
the bell tower.

**Solution.** The clues add up to a creature that loves shiny things and can reach
high places, and the glints lead to the top of the **old bell tower**. This is
observation and logic, not a magic, though **Starlight** can make a faint glint
easier to spot, **Riversong** can ask the square what it saw, and **Birdsong** can
ask the market birds which way the shiny little thief flew.

**Fallback.** If the children are stuck, have a market seller mention "that cheeky
bird up the tower has had my buttons again," handing them the link. Reaching the
answer with a nudge still counts as a full success and tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, lay out three shiny clue objects leading
in a line toward a tall "tower" (a stack of cups) and ask only "where do the shiny
things lead?"

## Stop 3: The Old Bell Tower (Normal)

**The challenge.** The shy jackdaw has the cup in her nest. She loves shiny things
and is easily startled. The players must befriend her, not snatch the cup.

**Intended solutions.** A befriending moment:
- **A fair shiny swap.** Offer her something that shines just as much (a carried
  trinket, a polished button) to take the cup's place in her nest.
- **A gentle voice.** Speak softly and move slowly so she is not frightened, and
  she will let you take it.
- **Starlight** to conjure a glint she finds even more delightful, or **Truth** to
  show her gently that the cup is needed and a swap is fair.
- **Birdsong** to speak to her in her own jackdaw chatter, so she stays calm and
  comes out instead of taking fright.

**Fallback.** If the children freeze, have the jackdaw herself nudge a shiny button
toward them, as if proposing the trade. The instant they offer any kindness or
swap, let her hop aside and release the cup.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, a single shiny swap is plenty; the
jackdaw is timid but easily pleased.

## Stop 4: The Cup Returned (Easy)

**The challenge.** The players return the cup and, harder, help the townsfolk undo
their unfairness and welcome the wrongly blamed traveller.

**Intended solution.** Have the players show the cup and explain there was no
thief, then say something that turns the crowd's shame into kindness: an
invitation, a shared loaf, a warm welcome for the traveller. There is no roll to
fail. **Truth** fits naturally here, but plain kind words work.

**Fallback.** If a child is unsure what to say, have the traveller smile and offer
forgiveness first, making it easy for the townsfolk to follow. The success is
guaranteed; this stop is about the warm feeling of fairness restored.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, simply handing the traveller a piece of
bread and saying sorry together is the whole of it.

## The Dawn Crow (no roll)

This is the resolution, not a puzzle, so there is no band and no roll. The cup goes
back on its cushion, the Galo de Barcelos crows at last, and the sun rises over
Barcelos. Let the children feel the market wake and the traveller leave happy.
They won by being gentle with the jackdaw and fair to a stranger, which is the
only way anyone wins among the Enchanted Springs.
