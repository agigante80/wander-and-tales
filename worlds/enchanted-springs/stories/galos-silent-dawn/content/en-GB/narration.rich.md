# The Galo's Silent Dawn

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause often so everyone can
puzzle, wonder, and try ideas. There are no wrong answers here, only new paths to
follow.*

## Before you begin

Barcelos wakes earlier than anywhere, because its rooster wakes it. But on the
morning this story begins, the sky over the cobbled square stayed a flat,
unbroken grey, and the market stood half-set and uneasy. The Galo de Barcelos,
the bright painted rooster who has crowed up every dawn for longer than anyone can
remember, sat hunched on his wall with his beak clamped shut. He had lost his
crow, and without it the morning simply would not come.

There was worse. The town's pride, the shining silver festival cup, had vanished
from its stall in the night, and the market folk, frightened and cross, had
fixed the blame on the easiest person: a weary traveller resting at the edge of
the square, guilty of nothing but being a stranger on the long road to Santiago.
He sat very still, saying little, waiting to be believed.

You looked at the silent rooster, and at the unhappy stranger, and you knew the
two troubles were really one. "Let us find the truth," you said, and shouldered
your bag.

*What is your hero name? Which three magics will you carry: Bloom, Truth,
Starlight, or Riversong?*

## Stop 1: The Silent Roost

You climbed to the Galo's wall, and close up he looked truly poorly: feathers
dull, head low, his bright comb drooping. He opened his beak to greet you and
nothing came out at all, not even a squeak. He could not crow, and so he could
not do the one thing he was famous for: crow out the honest truth and clear a
wrongly blamed soul. The unfairness of it had taken the heart right out of him.

A bird as proud as the Galo will not be poked or rushed. He needed gentleness, and
a reason to lift his head, before his voice would stir.

*How do you help the Galo find his heart again? Could Riversong fetch him a sip of
cool spring water, could Bloom bring a little colour back to his perch, or could
you simply sit with him and promise to set things right? What do you try?*

When the Galo felt truly cared for, he managed one small, scratchy cluck, the very
first thread of his voice returning. "Bring back the cup," he rasped, "lay it where
it belongs, and I will crow so loud the truth cannot be missed."

## Stop 2: The Barcelos Market

Down among the stalls, you studied the scene of the mystery. The silver cup's
velvet cushion sat empty, but everything around it was strange: no muddy
footprints, no forced lock, no sign that any thief had come or gone. A cup does
not simply walk away, and yet no person seemed to have carried it off.

Then you began to notice small things. A ribbon gone from one stall. A bright
button missing from another. And high above the square, near the top of the old
bell tower, something caught the weak light and winked.

*If no person took the cup, who did? What sort of creature loves a shiny thing,
can carry off ribbons and buttons, and can reach a high stone tower? Follow the
little thefts: where do they lead?*

When you laid the clues side by side, they all pointed the same way: up, past the
rooftops, to the open top of the old bell tower. You headed for its winding
stair.

## Stop 3: The Old Bell Tower

The stair climbed and climbed, and you came out breathless among the great bells,
with the swifts wheeling and the whole town small below. There, wedged in a snug
nest of buttons, ribbons, and bright glass, sat a small jackdaw with a neat grey
cap, and there, gleaming in the middle of her treasures, was the silver cup.

She was no thief, of course. She had only done what jackdaws do, gathering what
shines to make her nest beautiful, never dreaming of the worry it caused below.
She drew the cup close and watched you with one wary, glittering eye, ready to
startle at a loud word.

*How do you win the shy jackdaw's trust? She will not be grabbed at. Could a
gentle voice calm her, could Starlight offer a glint she likes even more, or could
you trade her something shiny she may keep? What would please a timid, treasure
loving bird?*

When you moved slowly and offered her a fair and shining trade, the jackdaw
hopped to one side and let you lift the cup from her hoard. She gave a soft,
pleased chirp, as if glad the worry was over too.

## Stop 4: The Cup Returned

You carried the silver cup back down into the square and raised it high for all to
see. "Here it is," you called. "It was never stolen. A little jackdaw took it up
to her nest in the tower, because it shone. There was no thief at all."

A hush fell over the market. One by one, the townsfolk turned to look at the
traveller they had been so quick to blame, and their faces filled with shame at
their own unfairness. It is a hard thing to face, having wronged a stranger.

*How do you help the market folk make it right? What words turn their shame into
kindness, and welcome the traveller properly at last?*

When the traveller was welcomed with warm bread, an apology, and a place by the
fire, the chill went out of the morning. Only one thing remained undone.

## The Dawn Crow

You set the silver cup back on its cushion, exactly where it belonged. The Galo de
Barcelos drew himself up tall, his comb bright red again, swelled his chest, and
this time, when he opened his beak, out rang a crow so clear and golden it seemed
to lift the very sun over the rooftops.

Light poured across the square. The stalls flung open, the bells answered, and the
traveller went on his way with a full heart and a fuller bag, blessing the town
that had learned to be fair. The Galo crows only the truth, and on this dawn the
truth turned out to be a kind one.

You did it together. You were gentle when it was easier to be quick, and you were
full of ideas when one idea was not enough. Well done.
