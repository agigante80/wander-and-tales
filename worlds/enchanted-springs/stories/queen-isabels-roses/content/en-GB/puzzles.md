# Queen Isabel's Roses: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players
need to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child
always moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward good ideas and
kindness over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Castle Kitchen (Easy)

**The challenge.** Take only a fair share of the rosca bread and carry it out
quietly, without waking the dozing cook or making the giving showy.

**Intended solutions.** A gentle planning moment:
- **Truth** to judge honestly how much is fair to take and no more.
- **Riversong** to hush a creaky door or a sloshing jug so nobody stirs.
- A plain careful plan: counting out just enough loaves, padding the basket with
  the cloth so it makes no sound, tiptoeing past the hearth.

**Fallback.** If the children hesitate, have a kitchen cat lead them to the
day-old loaves meant for sharing, settling the "how much is fair" question. Any
quiet, fair plan works and tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, just choosing two loaves and covering
the basket with a cloth is the whole of it.

## Stop 2: The Winding Streets (Normal)

**The challenge.** Work out, from small signs, who in the streets most needs the
bread, and give it so nobody feels ashamed.

**Solution.** The clues are the cold details: a chimney with no smoke, a thin cat
waiting, a pale child at a bare window. The players read these signs of need and
leave bread quietly at those doors, with a kind word and no fuss. This is
observation and empathy, not a magic, though **Starlight** can light a dim
doorway, **Bloom** can leave a small flower as a gentle, face-saving gift
beside the loaf, and **Birdsong** can send a little bird ahead to find the
quiet door where someone waits hungry.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have a neighbour murmur which house has
had no bread this week. Choosing kindly, even with a hint, is a full success and
tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, set out two doors, one warm and one
cold, and ask only which one needs the bread.

## Stop 3: The Watchman's Gate (Normal)

**The challenge.** The stern watchman blocks the gate and demands to see inside the
covered basket. The players must win him over with honesty and kindness, not a
lie and not a fight.

**Intended solutions.** A social, honest-heart moment:
- **Truth** is the natural fit: answer him plainly about the errand and the Queen's
  kindness, with nothing hidden.
- A warm, brave word: explain that the bread is for the hungry and ask for his
  blessing rather than his blame.
- **Bloom** or a shared loaf can thaw his mood and show good will.

**Fallback.** If the children freeze, have the watchman's own stomach rumble,
prompting a child to offer him a loaf, which softens him at once. Any honest,
gentle answer makes him relent and ask to see the basket.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, simply telling the watchman "it is bread
for people who are hungry" is enough to win him over.

## Stop 4: The Bread Becomes Roses (Easy)

**The challenge.** Lift the cloth and show the watchman the truth, trusting that
kindness done honestly will come right. There is no roll to fail; the miracle
rewards the children's honesty.

**Intended solution.** Have the players bravely lift the cloth to reveal the bread
has become roses. **Bloom** fits beautifully here, helping the roses open fuller,
but the wonder happens for any child who chose honesty at the gate. Let them enjoy
the watchman's astonishment.

**Fallback.** If a child is anxious about opening the basket, let the roses' scent
escape first, hinting that something lovely waits inside, so lifting the cloth feels
safe. The success is guaranteed.

**Make it simpler.** For a younger child, simply lifting the cloth and discovering
the roses together is the whole of it.

## Roses All the Way Home (no roll)

This is the resolution, not a puzzle, so there is no band and no roll. Roses bloom
through Coimbra, neighbours share what they have, and the won-over watchman offers
to help carry bread next dawn. Let the children feel the whole city soften and
Queen Isabel's quiet smile. They won by being honest and generous when no one was
watching, which is the only way anyone wins among the Enchanted Springs.
