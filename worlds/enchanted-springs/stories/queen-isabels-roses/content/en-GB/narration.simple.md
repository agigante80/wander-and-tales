# Queen Isabel's Roses

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

In the old city of Coimbra lived good Queen Isabel, who loved to carry bread to
people who were hungry. She did it quietly, because she did not want any thanks.

This morning the Queen is unwell and cannot go out. "Will you carry the kindness
for me?" she asks you gently. "Take some bread to those who need it, and do it
softly, the way I would."

You pick up your bag. "We will," you say. The city is waking up below the castle.

*What is your hero name? Which one or two magics do you choose: Bloom, Truth, Starlight,
Riversong, or Birdsong?*

## Stop 1: The Castle Kitchen

You slip into the warm castle kitchen. On the long table are golden loaves of
rosca bread, more than the castle could ever eat. The cook is dozing by the fire.

You want to take just enough to share, quietly, without waking anyone or taking
more than is fair.

*How many loaves do you take, and how do you carry them out softly? How do you
keep the kindness a secret? What do you try?*

When your basket is full and covered with a cloth, you tiptoe out into the
morning. Nobody stirs.

## Stop 2: The Winding Streets

You carry the covered basket down the narrow, winding streets. But who needs the
bread most? You look around carefully. A door with no smoke from its chimney. A
thin cat by a step. A small face at a cold window.

*Who do you think needs help this morning? How can you tell? How will you give the
bread so nobody feels ashamed?*

When you leave bread softly at the right doors, with a kind word and no fuss,
people smile in surprise. The streets feel warmer already.

## Stop 3: The Watchman's Gate

At the castle gate stands the watchman. He is big and stern, and he stops you.
"What is under that cloth?" he asks, frowning. "The King says the castle's bread
must not be given away."

He is not really unkind. He is only doing his job. But he wants to look in your
basket.

*What do you say to the watchman? Do you tell him the truth? How can you show him
that sharing bread is a kind and good thing?*

When you answer honestly and kindly, the watchman's frown begins to soften. "Show
me, then," he says, more gently now. "Lift the cloth."

## Stop 4: The Bread Becomes Roses

You take a deep breath and lift the cloth from the basket, ready to tell the whole
truth. But inside there is no bread at all.

The loaves have turned into roses. The basket is full of fresh white and pink
roses, spilling over the rim, even though it is not the season for them.

*How does that make you feel? What do you say? Can your Bloom magic help the roses
shine even brighter?*

The watchman stares, and his stern face breaks into a smile. There is nothing to
scold here, only flowers. The Queen's secret kindness is safe, wrapped up in a
small, sweet miracle.

## Roses All the Way Home

As you walk back, roses begin to bloom along the walls and in the doorways, all
through Coimbra. People come out to look and to share what they have. The watchman
himself picks up a basket to help carry bread the next morning.

Queen Isabel hears the happy noise from her window and smiles. Kindness given
quietly has filled a whole city with flowers.

You did it together. You were kind, and you were full of ideas. Well done.
