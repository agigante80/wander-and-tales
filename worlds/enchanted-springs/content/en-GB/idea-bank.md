# Idea Bank: The Enchanted Springs of Portugal

*Improv fuel for the grown-up. Dip in whenever you want a name, a nudge, or a surprise.
Nothing here is fixed; borrow what helps and invent the rest.*

## The four magics, in a child's words

- **Bloom:** "I make flowers and good things grow." Soften a hard moment, mend a torn
  net with vines, fill a sad room with blossom, calm a prickly mood.
- **Truth:** "I show what is really true." Like the Galo de Barcelos crowing, it points
  to the honest answer, the real culprit (always an innocent mix-up), the right path.
- **Starlight:** "I call a little light." Light a dark cave or a cloudy night, find a
  lost thing that glints, signal a friend across the valley.
- **Riversong:** "I talk with water and stones." Ask a spring where its water has gone,
  hear what an old wall remembers, calm a frightened creature with a soft hum.

## Friendly faces to drop in

- **The Moura Encantada** rewards a kind heart and is shy of loud or grabby hands.
- **The Galo de Barcelos** crows the truth, settles a quarrel, clears the wrongly blamed.
- **The Trasgo** hides shiny things and loves a game, a riddle, or a swap; never cruel.
- **Olharapo**, the big lonely giant, is gentle once someone is kind to him first.
- **Queen Isabel** is the spirit of quiet kindness: a loaf shared becomes a gift of roses.

## "What if?" sparks when the children get stuck or restless

- A bread left out turns to roses in the morning. Who left it, and for whom?
- A fountain has gone quiet. What did the water lose, and who might have it?
- A trail of something shiny leads off the path. Where does it go, and who is playful
  enough to have made it?
- A big shadow on the hill turns out to be lonely, not fierce. How do you make friends?
- A star is caught in the branches of an almond tree. How do you lift it gently home?

## Gentle reminders

- The "problem" is never a villain: a missing thing, a sad creature, a kind mix-up.
- More than one good idea always works. Reward fresh ideas and kindness over the
  "right" answer.
- When in doubt, let the water, a friendly creature, or a small light show the way.
