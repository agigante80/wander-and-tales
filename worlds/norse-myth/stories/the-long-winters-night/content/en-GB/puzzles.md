# The Long Winter's Night: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need
to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always
moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward imagination and kindness
over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Frozen Fjord (Normal)

**The challenge.** The heroes must cross the Frozen Fjord, a wide sheet of ice that
groans and creaks underfoot. The danger is not a monster but the ice itself, which
will not take a heavy, hasty crowd all at once.

**Intended solution.** This is a Spatial and Logic moment. The thoughtful, kind move is
to **spread out** so their weight is shared, **step gently** so they treat the ice
kindly, and **cross together holding hands** so no one is left behind. Laying the
**red yarn** down as a guide line, or testing the way ahead carefully, works just as
well.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have the **Two Ravens** wheel overhead and
"caw" the heroes into a careful, spread-out line, or let them spend an energy star to
steady their footing. Crossing slowly and gently always works, and tops up an energy
star.

**Make it simpler.** Tiptoe across the room together like quiet, careful penguins on
the slippery ice.

## Stop 2: The Lonely Frost Giant (Hard, then no roll)

**The challenge.** A huge giant of pale blue ice sits in the road with his arms
folded. He looks fearsome, and the players may feel this is a fight. It is not.

**Intended solution.** This is a Heart and Courage moment. The giant is simply cold
and lonely; nobody ever stops to speak with him. Any genuine kindness works: sharing
the **warm honey bun**, asking his name, offering him a place by the **Everwarm
Ember**, telling him he need not be alone. When the children are kind, his icy face
warms into a smile, he becomes their friend, and he comes along with them. There is
no roll to beat him, because there is no beating him; there is only befriending him.

**Fallback.** If the children freeze, or want to "win" by force, have the giant
shiver and ask, very quietly, "Are you cold and lonely too?" A single warm word
melts him. The kindness is the whole challenge, and it cannot be failed.

**Make it simpler.** Give the lonely giant a big warm hug and say "you can be our
friend".

## Stop 3: The Rainbow Bridge (Normal)

**The challenge.** The shimmering Rainbow Bridge rises to the high halls, and it holds
firm only for those who cross with a kind and honest heart. The heroes must cross,
carrying the ember, and reach the high hall to relight the world's warmth.

**Intended solution.** This is a Heart and Imagination moment. Because the heroes carry the
**Everwarm Ember** and now travel with the **Lonely Frost Giant** as a friend, their
hearts are exactly what the bridge is looking for. Crossing honestly and gently,
keeping their new friend beside them, the bridge brightens and holds. At the top they
lift the ember and the world's warmth catches light again.

**Fallback.** If they hesitate at the edge, have the bridge glow a little warmer with
each kind word they say, so a single honest "we came to bring the warmth back" lights
the way across. The bridge always holds for these heroes; their hearts have already
earned it.

**Make it simpler.** Walk a line of colourful cushions or a scarf laid on the floor,
holding hands and naming a colour on each step.

## The ending

There is no battle and no loser. When the ember is lifted at the high hall, every
hearth-fire relights down the valley, every amber window glows again, and the longest
night ends in dawn and colour. The **Aurora Spirits** dance overhead, the **Lonely
Frost Giant** has friends at last, and **Freya the Kind** smiles down from the high
halls. The heroes have won the only way anyone wins here, by being brave, kind, and
full of ideas.
