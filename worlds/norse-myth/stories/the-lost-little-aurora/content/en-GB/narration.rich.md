# The Lost Little Aurora

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause at each question so
the heroes can decide together. There are no wrong answers here, only braver ideas
to try.*

## Before you begin

In the deep of winter the night grows long in the fjords, and the long night is
meant to be the most beautiful time of all. High above the snow, the Aurora Spirits
dance, and the whole sky ripples with teal and green and purple light. But on this
particular evening, the sky stayed dark and still, as if it had forgotten how to
shine.

There was a reason. The littlest Aurora Spirit had tumbled down out of the sky and
fallen into the snowy Whispering Pines, and in her fall she had lost her colours.
Now she huddled somewhere among the dark trees, far too shy and far too cold to
glow, and without her the others would not dance.

Edda the Saga-Keeper called the two of you in from the cold, to the firelight of
the Hearth Hall. "You have bright ideas and kind hearts," she said, "and that is
worth more on a night like this than any sword. Will you go into the pines and help
the little aurora shine again?"

Into your hands she pressed three gifts: a little lantern to light your way, a ball
of bright red yarn, and a honey bun still warm from the hearth. "Remember," she
said, "up here we do not win by being the strongest. We win by being brave, kind,
and full of ideas."

*What is your hero name? Which quality will you carry: Imagination, Courage, or Heart?*

## Stop 1: The Dark Forest

You stepped out under the dark sky and into the Whispering Pines. The snow lay
deep and soft and hushed, and the tall trees rose around you, still and quiet, with
the wind carrying soft voices through their branches. Somewhere in all this
stillness, one tiny aurora was hiding, shy and cold and no bigger than a pair of
cupped hands.

She would be easy to miss. You would have to move gently and look with care.

*How will you find her? You might walk softly and watch for the faintest glimmer of
light, or follow small footprints pressed into the snow. Nori the Tomte, the
red-capped hearth sprite, is somewhere nearby, and he loves to help. What do you
try?*

When you slowed right down and looked kindly into the shadows, you caught it: a
soft, trembling glimmer behind a pine trunk. There she was, the littlest aurora,
dim and shivering and very pleased to be found. "Hello," you whispered. "We came to
help you." And she crept out to meet you.

## Stop 2: The Three Colours

The little aurora was glad beyond words to be found, but try as she might, she could
not make herself glow. "I lost my three colours when I fell," she said, and her
voice was small. "Teal, and green, and purple. They slipped away into the world,
and I cannot shine without them."

But the colours had not gone far at all. Each one was hiding somewhere close, in a
thing that wore that very colour, if only you knew where to look.

*Can you find all three of her colours? Where in this snowy place might teal be
hiding? Where green? Where purple? Think of the icicles, the pines, and the wide
evening sky.*

You found her teal in the heart of a shining icicle, her green in the deep needles
of a pine branch, and her purple high up where the twilight sky was turning dark.
One colour at a time, you helped her draw each one back into herself, and one colour
at a time, the little aurora began to glow, soft and warm and brighter every moment.

## Stop 3: The Climb to the Sky

She glowed now, gentle and lovely, but when she looked up at the great dark sky her
light wavered. "It is so very high," she said. "What if I cannot reach it? What if I
fall all over again?" Being found was one thing. Rising back up into the night was
quite another, and she was afraid.

She did not need to be braver than her fear. She only needed a little courage
borrowed from friends, and a hand to help her up.

*How will you help her find her courage? You might climb the tallest pine beside
her, cheer her on from below, or use the ball of red yarn to give her a gentle boost
up into the air. What is your plan?*

You climbed the tallest pine, branch by snowy branch, and tied the red yarn so she
had something to hold. Then you counted together, one, two, three, and called out,
"You can do it, we believe in you!" The little aurora drew a deep breath, let go of
the topmost bough, and leapt up into the dark.

## The Sky Dances Again

Up she went, and up, glowing brighter with every length she rose, until the other
Aurora Spirits came swooping down to meet her, laughing and singing, "You came back!
You came back to us!" They gathered her in among them as if she had never been
gone.

And then the whole sky woke. Teal and green and purple spilled across the night and
began to dance, ribbon over ribbon, swirling above the snowy fjord and the warm
amber windows of the longhouses below. The long night was dark no longer, and all
the fjords looked up in wonder.

High among the stars, Freya the Kind looked down and smiled. You had done it, and
you had done it together: with your ideas, with your courage, and above all with your
heart. Well done, heroes of the Snowlit Fjords of the North.
