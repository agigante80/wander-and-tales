# Ratatosk's Muddled Message

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause at each question so
the heroes can decide together. There are no wrong answers here, only braver ideas
to try.*

## Before you begin

The Great World Tree holds the whole world together. Its roots reach far down into
the deep dark earth, and its branches climb so high that an Eagle keeps a nest at
the very top, above the snowlit fjords. All day long, a quick red squirrel named
Ratatosk races up and down the trunk, carrying everyone's messages between the
roots and the sky. He has never dropped one in his life. Until today.

Today Ratatosk got into such a hurry that he muddled every message up. Because of
the mix-up, the great Eagle at the top of the tree now believes that a small friend
down among the roots said something unkind about him. And the small friend, hearing
the wrong words too, believes the Eagle said something unkind in return. Neither of
them ever did. It was only a tangle of crossed messages. But the two old friends
have turned their backs on each other, and the whole tree feels the chill of it.

Edda the Saga-Keeper called you to her side by the great fire of the Hearth Hall.
"You have clever heads and kind hearts," she said, "and that is worth more up there
than any axe. Will you help poor Ratatosk sort his messages and set things right,
so the two friends make up?"

Into your hands she pressed three gifts: a ball of bright red yarn, a snack to coax
a flustered squirrel, and a little lantern for the deep dark roots. "Remember," she
said, "in these fjords we do not win by being the strongest. We win by being
clever, brave, and kind."

*What is your hero name? Which quality will you carry: Wit, Courage, or Heart?*

## Stop 1: Catch the Squirrel

You stepped out beneath the Great World Tree, and there was Ratatosk, a streak of
red fur zooming up the trunk, round a branch, and back down again, far too flustered
to stop for anyone. "Sorry, sorry, no time, all muddled, can't stop!" he chattered
as he whizzed past in a blur.

He was in such a panic that he could not help a single soul, least of all himself.
Before anything could be mended, he needed to slow down, catch his breath, and rest.

*How might you help him feel calm? You carry a snack just for him. Could you sit
very still and speak in a soft, slow voice? Could you watch the way he runs and wait
patiently for the right moment to offer it?*

When you held the snack out gently and spoke low and kind, Ratatosk slowed, and
stopped, and finally sat. He nibbled, his quick little breaths grew steady, and his
racing heart settled at last. "Oh, thank you," he sighed. "It is so much easier to
think when somebody is kind to you. Now, to the muddle."

## Stop 2: Sort the Messages

Ratatosk opened his paws and out tumbled three little messages, all jumbled
together in a heap. Three friends were waiting along the tree, he explained: the
fierce-looking Eagle high at the very top, a wise old owl on a middle branch, and
the small friend far down among the roots. Each message belonged to just one of
them. But which message went to which friend? That was the muddle.

Down swooped the Two Ravens, Thought and Memory, to perch beside you. They fly over
all the fjords and forget nothing, and they do love to drop a helpful hint for
heroes who are truly trying.

*Can you remember who lives where on the tree? Read each message carefully and hunt
for the clue inside it. One message speaks of the wind and the wide sky up high. One
speaks of the cool deep dark down among the roots. One simply hoots a warm and
friendly hello. So which friend does each message belong to?*

When you matched every message to its right friend, the great tangle melted clean
away. "That's it! That's exactly it!" cheered Ratatosk, clapping his small paws
together and spinning on the spot. Now you knew precisely what to carry, and to
whom each kind, true message should go.

## Stop 3: Climb and Deliver

One task remained, and it was the most important of all. The right message had to
travel all the way up to the Eagle, and the right message all the way down to the
small friend in the roots, so that each of them could hear the true words at last
and understand.

The tree was very tall, and the way up was high and the way down was deep and dark.
But you had your ball of bright red yarn.

*How will you climb safely without losing your way? Could you tie the red yarn at
the bottom, like a safety line, and follow it up so you can always find the path
back? Could you light your little lantern to see down among the deep dark roots?*

When you tied on the red yarn and climbed slowly and carefully, you came at last to
the Eagle in his high nest. You read out the kind and honest message, the one that
had been his all along. The Eagle's fierce frown softened into wonder. "Oh," he
said quietly. "So my old friend was never unkind to me at all. It was only a
muddle." Then you followed the yarn back down, down, down to the roots, your little
lantern glowing, and you gave the small friend the true message too. The small
friend's eyes went round and bright and happy.

## Friends Again

High at the top of the Great World Tree, the Eagle spread his enormous wings and
waved warmly down the trunk. Far below in the roots, the small friend waved right
back up, and from top to bottom of the whole tree the two of them called out
together, "Friends again!"

Ratatosk raced one joyful loop around the entire tree, his tail streaming behind
him. "You sorted out my muddle and you mended my two friends," he said. "I will
never, ever forget it. Thank you, thank you, thank you!"

High above the snowlit fjords, where the aurora ripples teal and green and purple,
Freya the Kind looked down and smiled. You had done it, and you had done it
together: with your wit, with your courage, and above all with your kindness. Well
done, heroes of the Snowlit Fjords.
