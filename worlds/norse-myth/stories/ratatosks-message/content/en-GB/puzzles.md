# Ratatosk's Muddled Message: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need
to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always
moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward imagination and kindness
over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: Catch the Squirrel (Easy)

**The challenge.** Ratatosk the Squirrel is zooming up and down the Great World
Tree in a flustered panic, far too worked up to stop, listen, or help with anything.
The players need to calm him and coax him to rest before the muddle can be sorted.

**Intended solution.** This is a Heart and Observation moment. The kind move is to
slow right down themselves: sit still, speak softly, watch the way Ratatosk runs,
and at the right moment offer the **snack** Edda gave them. A patient, gentle child
who matches the squirrel's path and waits for him will win his trust quickly.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have Ratatosk pause for breath right
beside them so the snack is easy to offer, or let them spend an energy star to call
out something calming that he hears. Any genuine kindness settles him. Once he is
calm, he sits, nibbles, and thanks them, and the story moves on.

**Make it simpler.** Pretend to be a jittery squirrel together, then all take three
slow deep breaths and go still.

## Stop 2: Sort the Messages (Normal)

**The challenge.** Ratatosk tips out three jumbled messages. Three friends are
waiting on the tree, and each message belongs to exactly one of them. The players
must remember who lives where and match each message to the friend it belongs to,
using a clue tucked inside each one.

**The three friends.** Read them aloud so the children can picture the tree: **the
Eagle** lives at the very top, high in the wind and the wide sky; **the Owl** sits
on a middle branch, friendly and fond of a hello; and **the small friend** lives far
down among the deep dark roots.

**The three messages and their clues.** Read each message and let the children work
out who it belongs to:

- *"The wind is wild up here today, but I love the wide sky."* The clue is **the
  wind and the high sky**, so this message belongs to **the Eagle** at the top.
- *"It is cool and quiet and cosy down in the deep dark, just as I like it."* The
  clue is **the deep dark roots**, so this message belongs to **the small friend**
  in the roots.
- *"Whoo, hello, lovely to see a friend on my branch!"* The clue is the friendly
  **hoot** from a **branch**, so this message belongs to **the Owl** in the middle.

**Intended solution.** This is a Memory, Logic and Vocabulary moment. The good
move is to read each message, find the clue word (high sky, deep roots, a hoot), and
match it to the friend who fits. Matching all three sorts the muddle. The **Two
Ravens** are on hand to confirm a good guess or nudge a stuck one.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have a Raven read one message aloud again,
slowly, and ask "Now, who likes the wind up high?" Sorting even one message starts
the others falling into place, and getting there with the Ravens' help still counts
as a full success and tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** Use just two friends, the high Eagle and the low small friend,
and ask "high or low?" for each one.

## Stop 3: Climb and Deliver (Normal)

**The challenge.** The right message must travel all the way up to the Eagle and the
right message all the way down to the small friend, so each hears the true words and
the two understand each other again. The way up is high and the way down is deep and
dark.

**Intended solution.** This is a Spatial and Heart moment. The thoughtful, careful move
is to tie the **red yarn** at the bottom as a safety line and follow it up and back,
so the climbers can always find their way, and to light the **little lantern** for
the deep dark roots. Then comes the kind part: read each friend their true message
warmly, so the Eagle and the small friend each realise the unkind words were never
real, only a muddle.

**Fallback.** If the children feel the climb is too high, let Ratatosk scamper
alongside to show the safest branches, or let them spend an energy star to climb
hand in hand. If they are unsure what to say, the kind message can be as simple as
"Your friend was never cross with you; it was all a mix-up." The moment the true
words are spoken, the friend's frown turns to delight.

**Make it simpler.** Have the littlest one stretch up tall to reach the Eagle, then
crouch down low for the friend in the roots.

## The ending

There is no battle and no loser, because there was never a foe, only a tangle of
messages. When the right words reach the right friends, the Eagle high in the tree
and the small friend down in the roots wave warmly to each other and call out,
"Friends again!" Ratatosk thanks the heroes with a happy loop around the tree, and
**Freya the Kind** smiles down from above the snowlit fjords. The heroes have won
the only way anyone wins here, by being brave, kind, and full of ideas.
