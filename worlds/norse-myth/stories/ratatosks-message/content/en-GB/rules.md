# Ratatosk's Muddled Message: Rules for the Grown-Up

*New to this? Read the Guide for the Grown-Up first. It explains the whole idea in
two pages and you will never need it again.*

The Guide for the Grown-Up explains how the game works. This page holds only what is
special about this story. A failed roll is never a defeat; nobody loses. This is a
gentle tale of a mix-up mended with kindness, where good ideas and care win every
time. Nobody is unkind on purpose, and nothing here is ever truly broken, only
muddled and put right again.

## Setting up

- Print the map. Each player puts a small figure on the Start, the Hearth Hall.
- This world has no magic. Instead each hero leans on one of three qualities:
  **Imagination** (dreaming up ideas), **Courage** (a steady, warm heart), or **Heart**
  (caring for others). On the hero sheet, where it says magic, let each child write
  the quality they like best. Two different qualities work best together.
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). They are spent
  and refilled during play.
- Set out three props if you can: a ball of string for the red yarn, a small snack
  or token for the squirrel's snack, and anything at all for a lantern. Anything can
  be anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## The Twist of Fate

Once at each place the players may roll for a twist; read the matching line. The
Guide explains how it works.

| Roll | Twist of Fate |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nori the Tomte pops up and gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | You find your warm heart again, and take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A following wind down the tree, and the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | The Two Ravens, Thought and Memory, glide past and nod kindly. All is well. |
| 5 | A lucky knot in the red yarn: keep one roll to use again later. |
| 6 | A burst of hearth-warmth, and for a moment everyone feels brave. |

## How it ends

There is no foe in this tale at all, only a muddle. The fierce-looking Eagle and the
small friend in the roots are not enemies; they are old friends who have been hurt
by crossed messages. The story is won not by beating anyone but by sorting the
truth out and carrying the kind words to where they belong, so the two make up.
Times spent puzzling out who said what, and carrying a kind message to a friend, sit
happily alongside practice in memory, careful listening, and looking after others.
Let the moment when the friends wave to each other land warmly; it is the whole
point. See the puzzles page for the three challenges and their solutions.
