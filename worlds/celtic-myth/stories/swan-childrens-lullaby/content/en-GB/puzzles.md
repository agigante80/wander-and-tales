# The Swan Children's Lullaby: Puzzles and Solutions

*For your eyes, not the children's. Each stop lists its band, what the players need
to work out, the intended solution, and a gentle fallback so a stuck child always
moves forward. More than one good idea should work; reward imagination and kindness
over the "right" answer.*

## Stop 1: The Grey Mist (Easy)

**The challenge.** A thick grey mist hides the four swan children on the loch. The
players need to find them and draw near without frightening them away.

**Intended solution.** This is a Kindness and Observation moment. The good, kind
move is to slow right down: stand still and listen for a ripple or a soft feathered
sound, then call out gently and warmly so the swans know a friend has come. Loud or
sudden approaches make the swans shrink back into the mist; a calm, kind one brings
them gliding out.

**Fallback.** If the children are unsure, have a single white feather drift to their
feet, or let the smallest swan give a quiet call they can follow. Any gentle, kind
approach works, and once the swans see a friendly face they come close on their own.

**Make it simpler.** For a little one, play a quiet game of tiptoe-and-whisper: creep
softly and say "hello, swans" in your gentlest voice, and the swans glide out to meet
them.

## Stop 2: Remembering the Lullaby (Normal)

**The challenge.** The swan children have forgotten their lullaby. Three short
verses are drifting in the mist all out of order, and the song will not work until
the players put them back the right way.

**The verses.** Read them aloud in a jumbled order, then ask the children to sort
them. Number them only for yourself.

1. *Soft falls the dusk on the silver loch,*
   *little ones, rest your weary wings.*
2. *Up lifts the mist, all rose and gold,*
   *and the ring of old grey stones still sings.*
3. *Sleep now, sleep, till the morning sun*
   *wakes the world with the song it brings.*

**The ordering clue.** Tell the children the lullaby travels through one whole
night: it starts at dusk, rises through the dark, and ends at the morning sun. The
words that name the time of day give the order away: **dusk** comes first, then the
**mist lifting** in rose and gold, then **the morning sun** last. So the right order
is verse 1, then verse 2, then verse 3.

**Intended solution.** This is a Memory and Vocabulary moment. The players listen
for the time-of-day words (dusk, then the rising mist, then morning) and use them to
set the verses in order, dusk to dawn. Asking **Pook the Puca** for his hint works
just as well.

**Fallback.** If the children are stuck, have Pook the Puca hum the first line, or
point out the words "dusk" and "morning" and ask which one comes earlier in a night.
Getting there with help still counts as a full success and tops up an energy star.

**Make it simpler.** For a little one, use just two verses: read the bedtime one and
the morning one, and ask which we sing first when it is time to sleep.

## Stop 3: Playing It Together (Normal)

**The challenge.** The swan children know the words now but are too shy to sing, and
the smallest swan is shyest of all. She cannot find her voice on her own.

**Intended solution.** This is a Social-emotional and Courage moment. The players
lead the song rather than waiting for the swans: play the **little harp** to give
them a tune to follow, sing along so no swan has to begin alone, and offer the
shyest swan a kind word so she knows she is not on her own. When the players go
first and stay beside her, she finds her note and the others follow.

**Fallback.** If the children freeze, or wait for the swans to start, have the
shyest swan whisper, "Will you sing it with me?" The moment the players hum any tune
at all and invite her in, her first small note slips free and all four swan children
join the song.

**Make it simpler.** For a little one, hum "la-la-la" together and ask the shyest swan
to add just one "la" of her own; that single note is enough to start the whole song.

## The ending

There is no battle and no loser, and there never was a foe, only four shy swan
children who had lost their song. When the lullaby returns, the grey mist lifts into
rose and gold, the **Ring of Standing Stones** hums along, and **Danu the Kind**
smiles down on the loch. The heroes have won the only way anyone wins here, by being
brave, kind, and full of ideas.
