# The Swan Children's Lullaby

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only brave new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

Every dusk, four white swans sing a little song over the Misty Loch. Their lullaby
lifts the mist and paints the sky rose and gold. But tonight the song goes wobbly.
It forgets its tune. A sad grey mist settles over the loch and over the whole
village, and nobody can see the sunset.

Old Maeve the Tale-Weaver calls you to her by the warm hearth. "You two have heads
full of ideas and kind hearts," she says. "Will you help the swan children remember their
lullaby, so the mist lifts again?"

She gives you three gifts: the little harp, a sprig of oak, and a honey oatcake.
Then she smiles. "Here we do not win by being the strongest. We win by being
brave, kind, and full of ideas."

*What is your hero name? Are you a hero of Imagination, of Courage, or of Kindness?*

## Stop 1: The Grey Mist

You walk down to the loch. The grey mist is so thick you can barely see the water.
Somewhere out there are the four swan children, but they are quiet and hidden.

*How will you find them? Could you stand very still and listen? Could you call out
softly and kindly, so you do not frighten them? Try something and see.*

When you draw near, gently and kindly, four white shapes glide out of the mist. Four
swans with soft, sad eyes. They are pleased to see a kind face. They let you come
close.

## Stop 2: Remembering the Lullaby

The swan children want to sing, but they have forgotten their lullaby. The words
have all jumbled up. Three little verses float in the mist, out of order, and the
song will not work until they go back the right way.

Pook the Puca, a playful faerie, peeks out of an oak and gives you a wink and a
hint.

*Can you help them put the lullaby back together? Listen to each verse. Which one
comes first? Which one comes last? You can talk it through together.*

When you set the three verses in the right order, the swan children's eyes light up.
"That is it!" they whisper. "That is how it goes!"

## Stop 3: Playing It Together

The swan children know the words now, but they are shy. One little swan is the
shyest of all. She opens her beak, and no sound comes out. She tucks her head under
her wing.

*This is not a fight. How could you help her feel brave? You have the little harp.
Could you play a gentle tune for them all? Could you tell the shy swan she is not
alone, and sing along so she does not sing by herself?*

When you play the little harp and sing with them, the shyest swan lifts her head.
A small, sweet note slips out. Then another. Soon all four are singing together,
strong and clear.

## The Dusk Turns Rose

The lullaby rises over the Misty Loch, just as it used to. The grey mist softens
and thins. It melts away into rose and gold, and the sunset pours over the water.

Up on the green rise, the Ring of Standing Stones hums along, low and happy. The
swan children glide in a circle, singing their hearts out. And high above the loch,
Danu the Kind looks down and smiles at you.

You did it together. You were brave, you were kind, and most of all, you were
full of ideas. Goodnight, heroes. Sweet dreams.
