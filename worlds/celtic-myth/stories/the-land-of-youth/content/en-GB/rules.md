# The Journey to the Land of Youth: Rules for the Grown-Up

The Guide for the Grown-Up explains how the whole game works; read it first if this
is new. This page holds only what is special about this story. A failed roll is never
a defeat; nobody loses. This is a braver tale than some, set in the misty Otherworld
with its quiet pull to stay forever, but good ideas and kindness still win every time,
and no one is ever truly hurt.

## Setting up

- Print the map. Each player puts a small figure on the Start, the home loch shore.
- This world has no magic. Instead each hero leans on one of three qualities:
  **Imagination** (dreaming up ideas), **Courage** (a steady heart), or **Kindness**
  (caring for others). On the hero sheet, where it says magic, let each child write
  the quality they like best. Two different qualities work best together.
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). They are spent
  and refilled during play.
- Set out three props if you can: a twig or leaf for the sprig of oak, a small cake
  or token for the honey oatcake, and a ball of string or red wool. Anything can be
  anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## The Twist of Fate

Once at each place the players may roll for a twist; read the matching line. The
Guide explains how it works.

| Roll | Twist of Fate |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pook the Puca pops up and gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | You find your courage again, and take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A soft breeze drifts off the loch, and the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | The Salmon of Wisdom blows a lucky bubble. All is well. |
| 5 | A sprig of oak for luck: keep one roll to use again later. |
| 6 | A burst of birdsong, and for a moment everyone feels brave. |

## How it ends

There is no foe in this tale and nothing to defeat. The only danger is the gentle
pull of the Land of Youth, the temptation to stay forever in that lovely,
forgetful place. The story is won not by beating anyone but by remembering home: the
heroes gently help their half-forgetful friend recall the people and hearth waiting
for them, tie the red wool so all can find the way back, and choose, kindly and
together, to return across the mist. Let that choosing land; it is the whole point.
