# Pook's Tangled Paths

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only brave new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

It is nearly time for the May fair. But the winding paths of the Whispering Oak
Grove have gone all tangled. Soft ribbons of mist loop them over and round, and
now the village folk cannot find their way to the fair.

Who did this? Pook the Puca, a playful faerie who can change his shape. He did not
mean any harm. He only wanted a game.

Old Maeve the Tale-Weaver calls you to the Roundhouse. "You two have heads full of
ideas and kind hearts," she says. "Will you go and untangle the paths, and win Pook over
with a friendly word?"

She gives you three gifts: a ball of red wool, a honey oatcake, and a sprig of
oak for luck. Then she smiles. "Here we do not win by being the strongest. We win
by being brave, kind, and full of ideas."

*What is your hero name? Are you a hero of Imagination, of Courage, or of Kindness?*

## Stop 1: The Tangled Grove

You step into the grove. The paths loop and twist and double back on
themselves. You walk and walk, and somehow you keep coming back to the same old
oak tree.

It would be easy to get lost in here for ever.

*What could you do? You have a ball of red wool. Could you tie one end at the
start and follow it as you go? Then you can always find your way back.*

When you tie the red wool at the start and unspool it behind you, you never lose
your way. The grove cannot tangle a hero with a thread. Soon you find the path
that leads deeper in.

## Stop 2: Pook's Three Doors

The path opens into a clearing. There, Pook has grown three mossy doors out of
oak branches, side by side. Only one door leads on. The others loop you straight
back.

Pook pops up, a little furry shape with bright eyes, and gives you three teasing
clues:

*"The left door loves the morning sun, but it sends you back where you begun.
The middle door is mossy and old, and it keeps a fib for every tale it told.
The right door is quiet and small and true, and that is the door that will see
you through."*

*Which door is the true one? Could you read his clues again, slowly? Could you ask
him for a hint?*

When you choose the right-hand door, it swings open with a happy creak. Pook
giggles and somersaults away into the leaves. You step on through.

## Stop 3: Winning Pook Over

Deeper in the grove you find Pook again, sitting on a toadstool. He has stopped
laughing now. He hugs his knees and looks at his feet.

He made the whole grove into a game, and now he feels a little bit sorry. He did
not think the village folk would be sad.

*This is not a fight. What kind thing could you do? You have a honey oatcake to
share. You could sit beside him. You could tell him the fair has room for him too.
What do you try?*

When you are kind to him and share the honey oatcake, Pook's eyes go warm and
bright. "You are not cross with me?" he whispers. "Then I will set it all right."

## The Paths Run True

Pook hops up and claps his furry paws. All across the grove, the ribbons of mist
unwind and float away. The winding paths run straight and true again.

And Pook does not vanish this time. He skips along beside you, leading the way,
proud to help at last. Down in the village, the folk cheer as the paths open, and
the May fair begins with music and dancing.

High above the green isles, Danu the Kind looks down and smiles. You did it
together. You were brave, you were kind, and you were full of ideas. Well
done, heroes.
