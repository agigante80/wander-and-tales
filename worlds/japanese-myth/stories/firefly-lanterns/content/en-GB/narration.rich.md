# The Firefly Lanterns

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause at each question so
the heroes can decide together. There are no wrong answers here, only braver ideas
to try.*

## Before you begin

Tonight was the night of the summer Lantern Festival, the warmest night of all the year
in the Lantern Village. Every summer the paper lanterns came alive with light, and the
whole village danced beneath them, the little ones chasing each other between the glowing
rows while the drums beat soft and slow. But this year, as dusk settled over the
terraced rice fields, the lanterns hung grey and dark, and not a single one would light.

The reason was a gentle one, and a little sad. Inside each paper lantern lived a shy
Firefly Spirit, and it was their soft light that made the lanterns glow. Tonight, every
one of them had slipped away to hide. They had flown up into the Whispering Bamboo Grove,
deep among the dark stalks, far too timid to come out and shine.

Granny Yuki the Tale-Keeper, who knew every story the mountains had ever told, called the
two of you to her side. "You have wise heads and kind hearts," she said, "and that is
worth more on a night like this than any lantern-pole. Will you climb to the grove and
coax the firefly spirits gently home, so our festival can glow again?"

Into your hands she pressed three gifts: a round paper lantern on a little handle, a ball
of bright red thread, and a soft, sweet rice cake wrapped in a leaf. "Remember," she
said, her eyes crinkling, "up in these hills we never win by being loud or strong. We win
by being wise, brave, and kind."

*What is your hero name? Which quality will you carry tonight: Wisdom, Courage, or
Kindness?*

## Stop 1: The Dark Bamboo

The path wound up through the cool evening to the Whispering Bamboo Grove, where the tall
green stalks clicked and swayed and whispered together in the wind. Inside it was dark
and very, very still. Here and there a tiny light blinked on, then vanished, then blinked
somewhere new, like stars that could not decide whether to come out. The firefly spirits
were close. But they were shy, and the smallest sound sent them flickering away.

From the green shadows stepped a russet fox with bright, clever eyes and a single leaf
balanced on his head. It was Kon the Fox, who loved a good game and a kind heart, and he
gave you a slow, friendly wink, as if to say there was no hurry at all.

*The spirits are timid, and timid friends cannot be chased. What gentle thing could you
try? Could you move slowly and keep your voices soft? You carry a sweet rice cake to
offer. Could you simply sit still and patient, and let the little lights choose to come
to you?*

So you grew quiet and calm, and waited. One small light drifted near, then hovered, then
settled. Another followed, and another, until a whole soft cloud of golden fireflies
floated all around you, warm against your cheeks, no longer the least bit afraid. "Well
done," whispered Kon, his tail curling with pleasure. "They trust you now. They will
follow where you lead."

## Stop 2: The Lantern Path

The fireflies would come home with you, the fox explained, but only if every step felt
safe. A long path of empty paper lanterns ran down the hillside to the village, and if
you could light them in the right order, a gentle road of light would open up and lead
the shy spirits all the way down.

But in what order? The lanterns stood in many sizes, some no bigger than a teacup and
some as round as the moon, and your ball of red thread ran from one to the next, tying
the whole line softly together.

*How will you light them so the spirits never take fright? You might go from the smallest
lantern to the very biggest, a path that grows as gently as the dawn. You might simply
follow the red thread from lantern to lantern. Or you might ask Kon the Fox, who knows
these paths well, for a quiet hint. What is your plan?*

When you found the gentle order and touched each lantern alight, one after another, a
soft golden road unrolled down the hill. The little fireflies bobbed along it in a happy
ribbon of light, following close behind you, every step of the way a step they were not
afraid to take.

## Stop 3: The Great Lantern

At the top of the festival square stood the Great Lantern, taller than a grown-up and
rounder than a barrel, the heart of the whole festival. To light it, the very shyest
firefly of all had to fly up inside and shine. But this little spirit was the most timid
of them all. It hung back at your shoulder, trembling, gazing up at the great dark
lantern as if it were a mountain too high to climb.

This was never a fight, and there was nothing here to beat. The little firefly only
needed to find, somewhere inside itself, a small and steady scrap of courage.

*What could you say to help it feel brave? You might speak in the softest, calmest voice
you have. You might tell it that you believe in it, and that you will be right here
watching the whole time. You might gently remind it how brave it has already been. What
do you try?*

When you cheered it on, warm and unhurried, the little firefly drew in a tiny breath,
lifted from your shoulder, and flew up into the dark. For a heartbeat there was nothing.
Then the Great Lantern bloomed with light, the warmest and brightest of them all, and the
little firefly glowed at its very heart, no longer afraid.

## The Festival Glows

One by one, all across the village, every lantern caught the light and glowed a deep,
happy gold, until the dark was chased clean away. The firefly spirits swirled out into
the warm night air and danced, looping and spinning in bright circles above the rooftops,
not a shy bone left among them.

The whole village burst into cheers and laughter, and the dancing began. The drums beat
soft and warm, the lanterns swayed like a low sky full of small, kind moons, and Kon the
Fox watched from a rooftop with his eyes shining.

And when at last the morning came, warm Amaterasu the Sun-Kami rose gently over the
blossom mountains and smiled down upon the valley. You had done it, and you had done it
together: with your wisdom, with your courage, and above all with your kindness. Well
done, heroes of the blossom mountains.
