# The Firefly Lanterns: Rules for the Grown-Up

*New to this? Read the Guide for the Grown-Up first. It explains the whole idea in
two pages and you will never need it again.*

You are the Game Master, which here means a few small jobs at once: storyteller,
gentle referee, the children's biggest fan, and a fellow adventurer who plays along.
This is a cooperative game for two or more who play together, a grown-up and one or
more children, all on the same side. Nobody competes, and nobody loses. This is a
gentle tale, soft and warm from start to finish, and no one is ever frightened or hurt.

## Setting up

- Print the map. Each player puts a small figure on the Start, the Lantern Village.
- This world has no magic. Instead each hero leans on one of three qualities:
  **Wisdom** (calm, clever thinking), **Courage** (a steady heart), or **Kindness**
  (caring for others). On the hero sheet, where it says magic, let each child write the
  quality they like best. Two different qualities work best together.
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). They are spent
  and refilled during play.
- Set out three props if you can: a paper cup or anything round for the paper lantern,
  a ball of red string or wool for the red thread, and any small token for the rice
  cake. Anything can be anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## Difficulty bands, not dice

Challenges never call for a particular die. Each one has a band: **Easy**,
**Normal**, or **Hard**. When the players try something, look up the band on the
kit's dice-band table, which tells you the number to meet or beat with whatever
dice your family owns. Every challenge can be played with a single ordinary die.

## The golden rule: here nobody loses

A failed roll is never a defeat. It is a detour to a different route. When a roll
does not meet the band, the players always have a way through:

- **Spend an energy star** to gather themselves and try the idea again, or
- **Join two qualities** once per stop to pass with no roll at all (Wisdom and
  Kindness together, say, or Courage and Wisdom).

Energy stars come back when the players move on to the next stop, and when they
help each other. Nobody can ever run dry for long, and nobody is ever stuck.

## Helping each other: the Helping Hand

At any moment one player may help another instead of acting alone. When they do,
make the help roll: on a success the challenge is passed for the pair, even if the
first roll missed. Helping is generous, so reward it; it also tops up an energy
star. On a night like this, no hero shines alone.

## The Twist of Fate

At each stop, once, the players may roll for a twist and you read out the matching
line. Use whichever die the dice-band table gives you for this; with a single
ordinary die, read the line that matches the number rolled.

| Roll | Twist of Fate |
|---|---|
| 1 | Kon the Fox slips from the bamboo and gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | You find your calm again, and take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A soft breeze drifts down the hill, and the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | The Guardian Lion-Dogs nod kindly from the gate. All is well. |
| 5 | A lucky cherry petal lands on your shoulder: keep one roll to use again later. |
| 6 | The soft ring of a temple bell, and for a moment everyone feels calm and brave. |

## How it ends

The shyest little firefly at the Great Lantern is the last "challenge", but it is no
foe. It is simply a small spirit that feels too timid to shine. The tale is won not by
pushing it but by encouraging it with a calm and kindly word, and the festival blazes
into light. Let the kindness land; it is the whole point. See the puzzles page for the
three challenges and their solutions.
