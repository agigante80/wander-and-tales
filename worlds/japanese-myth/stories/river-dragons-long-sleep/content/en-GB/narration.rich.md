# The River Dragon's Long Sleep

*Read aloud, or let an older child read it themselves. Pause at each question so
the heroes can decide together. There are no wrong answers here, only braver ideas
to try.*

## Before you begin

All through the long, dry summer the rains had not come. The Silver River, which
fed every terraced field in the valley, sank lower week by week, until the young
rice stood pale and thirsty and the elders watched the cloudless sky with worried
faces.

For high on the mountain, in a deep bend of the river, slept the Gentle River
Dragon, whose slow stirring had brought the rains since before anyone could
remember. This year he had slept far too long, and no one knew how to wake him.

Granny Yuki the Tale-Keeper called the two of you to her side. "You have heads
full of ideas and kind hearts," she said, "and that is worth more up the mountain than any
strength. Will you climb over the pass and wake him, gently, before the harvest
fails?"

Into your hands she pressed three gifts: a little bronze bell with a clear, soft
ring, a ball of bright red thread, and a sweet rice cake wrapped in a leaf.
"Remember," she said, "up there we do not win by being the strongest. We win by
being brave, kind, and full of ideas."

*What is your hero name? Which quality will you carry: Imagination, Courage, or
Kindness?*

## Stop 1: The Guardian Gate

The path climbed steeply through the cedars until it reached the Red Torii Gate,
tall and vermilion against the green of the mountain. On either side crouched a
great stone lion-dog, so still you might have walked straight past them. Then, as
you drew near, the stone seemed to soften: they blinked, they stretched, and they
turned their carved heads to look at you.

"None may pass," the Guardian Lion-Dogs rumbled together, "until you answer our
riddle." Their voices were deep as a temple drum, and for a moment your heart beat
quick. But there was no cruelty in their eyes, only a fair and patient sort of
watchfulness. They would have liked a good idea, and they would not have minded
a brave, thoughtful guess.

*Will you listen closely and puzzle it out together? Kon the Fox is somewhere near
the bamboo, and he loves to whisper a hint. What do you try?*

When your answer came, the Guardian Lion-Dogs nodded slowly and settled back onto
their pedestals. "Well thought," they said, and a low, pleased rumble followed you
as the great gate swung open and let you through.

## Stop 2: The Moonlit Bridge

Beyond the gate the daylight faded, and a round silver moon rose over the river.
There the path met an old arched bridge of dark wood, beautiful and a little
frightening, for it swayed and groaned whenever the wind pushed at it. The water
ran fast and black far below.

You understood, almost at once, what the bridge asked of you. It held firm only for
those who crossed it slowly and calmly, together: no rushing, no fear-driven
scramble, just one steady step after another, with a brave and quiet heart.

*How will you cross? Will you go gently, side by side, breathing slow? You might tie
the red thread between you so that you stay together all the way. What is your
plan?*

By crossing slowly and calmly, hand in hand, you kept the bridge steady beneath
your feet. It carried you the whole long arc above the water, and set you down at
last on the far bank, safe in the drifting mist.

## Stop 3: The Sleeping Dragon

And there, at last, lay the great River Dragon. He was curled along a deep, misty
bend of the river, his long body vast as a green hill, his breath rising and
falling slow as the tide. He had slept so very long that mist had gathered on his
scales and reeds had grown between his claws. He looked, for a moment, like the end
of the adventure.

But this was never a fight, and could never be one. You could no more battle such a
creature than battle the mountain itself, and you would not have wished to. He was
not fierce. He was gentle, and, you slowly understood, he was lonely, for no one had
climbed all this way to sit with him in a very long time.

*This calls for a calm and careful kindness. You carry a little bell that rings soft
and clear, the kind of sound that wakes a friend without a fright. You could ring it
gently and sing him a quiet song. What do you do?*

When you rang the little bell and sang, soft and low, the great dragon's sleepy eyes
drifted open, deep and dark and kind. "Someone came," he murmured, as though he
could scarcely believe it. "Someone came to wake me kindly, and not with shouting."
And he smiled, slow as sunrise.

## The Rains Return

The River Dragon uncurled and rose, grand and unhurried, into the moonlit sky. He
wound above the valley like a long blue ribbon, and everywhere his shadow passed, a
soft, warm rain began to fall, the first rain of the whole long summer.

The Silver River swelled and brimmed and shone. Far below, the thirsty rice fields
drank deep and turned, almost as you watched, a glad and living green. And the
dragon was lonely no longer, for now he had two friends who would climb the mountain
to see him again.

As the clouds thinned, Amaterasu the Sun-Kami broke through with a warm gold light
and smiled down upon you. You had done it, and you had done it together: with your
imagination, with your courage, and above all with your kindness. Well done, heroes of
the Blossom Mountains.
