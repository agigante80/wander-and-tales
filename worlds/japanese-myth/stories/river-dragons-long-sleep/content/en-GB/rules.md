# The River Dragon's Long Sleep: Rules for the Grown-Up

*New to this? Read the Guide for the Grown-Up first. It explains the whole idea in
two pages and you will never need it again.*

The Guide for the Grown-Up explains how the whole game works. This page holds only
what is special about this story. This is a braver tale than some, with a long climb,
a swaying bridge by moonlight, and a great sleeping dragon, but imagination and
kindness still win every time, and no one is ever truly hurt. A failed roll is never a
defeat; nobody loses.

## Setting up

- Print the map. Each player puts a small figure on the Start, the Lantern Village.
- This world has no magic. Instead each hero leans on one of three qualities:
  **Imagination** (dreaming up ideas), **Courage** (a steady heart), or
  **Kindness** (caring for others). On the hero sheet, where it says magic, let each
  child write the quality they like best. Two different qualities work best together.
- Give each player five energy stars (five small tokens or bricks). They are spent
  and refilled during play.
- Set out three props if you can: a small bell or anything that makes a soft sound,
  a ball of string for the red thread, and a small cake or token for the rice cake.
  Anything can be anything.
- Keep your dice and the dice-band table from the back of the kit beside you.

## The Twist of Fate

Once at each place the players may roll for a twist; read the matching line. The
Guide explains how it works.

| Roll | Twist of Fate |
|---|---|
| 1 | Kon the Fox slips from the bamboo and gives you one free hint. |
| 2 | You find your calm again, and take back one energy star. |
| 3 | A soft breeze drifts down the mountain, and the next challenge is Easy. |
| 4 | The Guardian Lion-Dogs nod kindly from afar. All is well. |
| 5 | A lucky cherry petal lands in your hand: keep one roll to use again later. |
| 6 | The soft ring of a temple bell sounds far off, and everyone feels calm and brave. |

## How it ends

The Gentle River Dragon at the misty river is the great "obstacle", but he is no
villain at all. He is not angry, and he is not wicked; he is simply a kind old
creature who has slept far too long and grown a little lonely. The tale is won not
by force or by shouting, which would only frighten him, but by waking him gently and
kindly, with the soft little bell and a calm, friendly song. Let that gentleness
land; it is the whole point. See the puzzles page for the three challenges and their
solutions.
