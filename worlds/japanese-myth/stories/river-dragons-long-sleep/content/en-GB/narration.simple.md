# The River Dragon's Long Sleep

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only brave new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

The Silver River is running low. All summer the rains have not come, and the
terraced rice fields are growing thirsty. The grown-ups in the village look up at
the dry blue sky and shake their heads.

Far up the mountain sleeps the Gentle River Dragon. When he stirs, the rains
come. But he has slept far, far too long this time, and the river keeps sinking.

Granny Yuki the Tale-Keeper calls you to her side. "You two have heads full of ideas and
kind hearts," she says. "Will you climb over the mountain and wake him, gently,
before the harvest fails?"

She gives you three gifts: a little bronze bell, a ball of red thread, and a sweet
rice cake. Then she smiles. "Up there we do not win by being the strongest. We win
by being brave, kind, and full of ideas."

*What is your hero name? Are you a hero of Imagination, of Courage, or of Kindness?*

## Stop 1: The Guardian Gate

You climb the steep path and come to the Red Torii Gate. On each side sits a big
stone lion-dog, still as rock. As you step close, they blink and stretch.

"None may pass," they rumble, "until you answer our riddle." They look fierce, but
their eyes are kind. They like a good thinking face, and a brave guess is welcome.

*What could you do? Could you listen closely and puzzle it out together? You could
ask Kon the Fox for a hint. A thoughtful guess is allowed too. What do you try?*

When you answer, the Guardian Lion-Dogs nod and lean back. "Well thought," they
say. The gate swings open, and the path leads on.

## Stop 2: The Moonlit Bridge

Night is falling now. Ahead, an old wooden bridge arches over the river, silver in
the moonlight. It looks lovely, but it sways and creaks when you hurry.

You can feel it. This bridge only holds firm for those who cross it slowly and
calmly, together, with a steady heart.

*How will you cross? Could you go gently, hand in hand, taking a deep breath? Could
you tie the red thread so you stay together? What is your plan?*

When you cross slowly and calmly, side by side, the bridge holds firm under your
feet. You step off the far end, safe, into the misty dark.

## Stop 3: The Sleeping Dragon

At last you reach the wide, misty river. And there he is: the great River Dragon,
curled along the water, vast as a hill, breathing slow. He has slept for so long
that mist has gathered on his back.

This is not a fight. You could never wake such a giant by shouting, and you would
not want to frighten him. He is gentle, and a little bit lonely.

*What kind, calm thing could you do? You have a little bell that rings soft and
clear. You could ring it and sing him a quiet, friendly song. What do you try?*

When you ring the little bell and sing softly, the great dragon's sleepy eyes open.
"Someone came," he murmurs, surprised and glad. "Someone came to wake me kindly."

## The Rains Return

The River Dragon uncurls and rises, slow and grand, into the night sky. He winds
above you like a long blue ribbon, and where he passes, soft rain begins to fall.

The Silver River brims and shines. Down in the valley, the thirsty rice fields
drink deep and turn green again. The dragon is not lonely now, for he has friends.

High above, Amaterasu the Sun-Kami breaks through the clouds and smiles down on
you. You did it together. You were brave, you were kind, and most of all, you were
full of ideas. Well done, heroes.
