# The Tanuki's Tidy-Up

*Read aloud, slowly and warmly. Stop often to let the children answer. There are
no wrong answers here, only kind new ideas to try.*

## Before you begin

It is festival day in the Lantern Village. All morning the market has been getting
ready, with stalls of paper fans, baskets of fruit, and rows of glowing lanterns.
But oh dear. Pon the Tanuki was so excited that he bounced and rolled right through
the market, and he knocked every stall over by mistake. Now the fans, the fruit,
and the lanterns are all in one big jumble, and Pon feels terrible.

Granny Yuki the Tale-Keeper calls you to her. "You two have clever heads and kind
hearts," she says. "Will you help Pon set the market right before the festival
opens?"

She gives you three gifts: a ball of red thread, a little bell, and a sweet rice
cake. Then she smiles. "Up here we do not win by being the strongest. We win by
being wise, brave, and kind."

*What is your hero name? Are you a hero of Wisdom, of Courage, or of Kindness?*

## Stop 1: The Jumbled Stalls

You hurry to the market and find Pon the Tanuki sitting in the middle of the mess.
His ears droop. His nose wobbles. "I have spoiled everything," he sniffs. "I only
wanted to help."

He is too upset to tidy up. He needs a kind word first.

*What could you do to help Pon feel better? You could sit beside him. You could tell
him it was an accident. You could say that everyone can fix a muddle together. What
do you try?*

When you are kind to Pon, he wipes his nose and gives a little smile. "You are good
friends," he says. "Let us tidy up together." And he jumps up, ready to help.

## Stop 2: The Great Sort

Now you look at the jumble. Fans, fruit, and lanterns are all mixed up in one big
heap. Everything must go back to the right stall before the festival opens.

The fans belong on the fan stall. The fruit belongs in the fruit baskets. The
lanterns belong on the lantern posts. You will need to sort them, and count them,
and match them up.

*How will you sort it all out? Could you make a pile for each kind of thing? Could
you count the lanterns onto the posts? Could you match the fruit by its colour? What
is your plan?*

When you sort and count it all back into place, the market begins to look right
again. Pon claps his paws. "You are so clever!" he says.

## Stop 3: The Last Lantern

The market is nearly perfect. But one lantern is still missing: the big red festival
lantern that hangs over the very middle of the market. Without it, the festival
cannot begin.

Then Kon the Fox trots out of the bamboo. "I saw something round and red roll under
the plum tree by the bridge," he says with a wink. "Follow my clue."

*Where could the last lantern be? Could you follow Kon's clue to the plum tree? Could
you ask Pon to help you look? What do you do?*

You follow the clue and there it is, the big red lantern, safe under the plum tree.
You carry it back and hang it high. "I found the spot it rolled to," says Pon
proudly. "I helped after all." And he stands a little taller.

## The Festival Opens

The market is set right at last. Every fan is fanned out, every basket of fruit is
neat, and every lantern glows warm in its place, with the big red one shining in the
middle.

Pon the Tanuki is forgiven, and best of all, he is proud. The drum begins to beat,
the people come laughing into the square, and the festival opens at last.

High above, Amaterasu the Sun-Kami looks down and smiles. You did it together. You
were wise, you were brave, and most of all, you were kind. Well done, heroes of the
Lantern Village.
