# Marketing paragraph (drafts), positioning, and the evidence behind them

Drafted in the founder's voice, grounded in the sources in this folder and in the
landscape scan (`../landscape/comparison-and-ideas.md`). The drafts deliberately use
careful, associational language and avoid causal or "makes kids smarter" claims, and
they now lead with what actually sets this project apart: its niche. Pick one, or mix.

## What makes it different (the niche)

One line: **a free, printable, cooperative, no-lose story-adventure library for a
grown-up and one or more children, written and checked by people, that grows with
your child's reading and plays in your family's language.**

This is the honest, defensible space the landscape scan found nobody else occupies all
at once. State it plainly:

- **Free and printable.** Download a kit, print it at home, and play. No app, no
  subscription, no credits, no parts to lose or replace. (This is also a direct answer
  to the loudest complaints in the digital corner: subscription traps and shipping
  delays.)
- **Cooperative and no-lose.** You and your child (or children) play on the same side,
  and the grown-up plays along too, as a fellow adventurer who helps rather than a
  referee apart. Nobody loses, and a tricky moment is a detour to another way, never a
  defeat.
- **No screen, and no AI in your child's path.** Every story is written and checked by
  a person. There is no chatbot for your child to talk to, and nothing is invented on
  the fly in front of them.
- **It grows with your child.** Each story comes at two reading levels (read-aloud for
  the youngest, richer prose for confident readers) across three age tiers, so one
  world can last for years.
- **In your family's language, properly.** Ready to play in British English and Spanish
  from Spain, with Italian on the way, each written with care rather than
  machine-translated breadth.

Together these are simply the screen-free, attentive, together time the research keeps
pointing to, made easy to start.

## Short version

> No one can say exactly what the world will look like for our children as AI
> reshapes it. But the people who study it agree on the human skills that will
> matter most: imagination, creative and critical thinking, problem-solving,
> curiosity, and knowing how to work well with someone else. Wander & Tales is a
> simple way to nurture exactly those, away from a screen. It is a free, printable,
> cooperative story-and-puzzle adventure you play together with your child or children,
> with a single die and a few household bits, where nobody loses and the only way
> forward is
> kindness and good ideas. Every story is written and checked by people, not generated
> by a chatbot in your child's hands, and it comes ready to play in British English
> and Spanish from Spain (with Italian on the way), at a reading level that grows with
> your child. And the research keeps returning to one thing above all: attentive,
> screen-free time between a parent and child is good for how children grow. This is a
> lovely excuse for more of it.

## Longer version (leans into the "unknown future" feeling)

> We are raising a generation into a world none of us can fully picture. As AI takes
> over more of the analyzing and the answering, the things that will set our kids
> apart are the ones that stay stubbornly human: imagination, creative and critical
> thinking, curiosity, the instinct to think outside the box, and the patience to work
> something out together with another person. The World Economic Forum and the OECD
> say much the same, naming analytical and creative thinking, problem-solving,
> resilience, and cooperation as the capacities the next generation needs most. We
> cannot know what the AI world will become. But we do know that imaginative,
> cooperative play exercises those very muscles, and that the most consistent positive
> finding in all the screen research is simply a parent and child paying attention to
> each other, away from a screen. Wander & Tales is built for that moment: a free,
> printable, cooperative adventure you play around a table with a single die and a few
> household objects, where you and your child solve puzzles with imagination instead of
> buttons, where nobody loses, and where you win by being kind and full of ideas. There is no
> app, no screen, and no chatbot talking to your child: every story is written and
> checked by a person, and each one comes in careful British English and Spanish from
> Spain (Italian is on the way), at two reading levels so the same world can grow with
> your child. A simple way to stretch how your little one thinks, and a lovely way to
> spend an afternoon together.

## Evidence notes (which claim rests on what)

- "the skills that will matter most ... imagination, creative and critical
  thinking, problem-solving, curiosity, cooperation": WEF Future of Jobs 2025 and
  OECD Learning Compass 2030. See `02-skills-for-an-ai-future.md`. [verified]
- "the most consistent positive finding ... attentive, screen-free time between a
  parent and child": the co-use result in Mallawaarachchi et al. 2024 (JAMA
  Pediatrics, 176,742 children), where co-use was positively associated with
  cognitive outcomes and distracted caregiver device use with poorer psychosocial
  outcomes. See `01-screens-ai-and-children.md`. [verified] Phrase as
  "associated with", not "causes".
- "imaginative, cooperative play exercises those very muscles": pretend-play and
  board-game studies in `03-play-and-development.md`. [fetched] Supportive but
  the weakest-evidenced pillar; keep the verb soft ("exercises", "practises",
  not "builds" or "boosts").
- "we cannot know what the AI world will become": matches UNICEF and WEF, which
  state the long-term effects of AI on children are largely unknown. See
  `01-screens-ai-and-children.md`, section B. This humility is accurate, not just
  rhetorical.

## Positioning notes (which niche claim rests on what)

These are product-true statements about Wander & Tales, reinforced by the landscape
scan. They are facts about the project, not research findings.

- "free, printable, no subscription, no parts to lose": product fact, and a direct
  answer to the documented complaints in the digital corner (subscription and
  cancellation traps, costly credits, shipping friction) and the printed corner
  (single-use components, lost pieces). See `../landscape/comparison-and-ideas.md`.
- "cooperative and no-lose": product design, and a proven, sought-after category
  (Peaceable Kingdom's everyone-wins design, Amazing Tales' failure-forward
  narration). See the landscape notes.
- "no screen, and no AI in your child's path; written and checked by people": product
  fact, and a counter to the hallucination and safety concerns in the digital corner
  (AI Dungeon, character.ai). See the honesty caveat below before phrasing it.
- "grows with your child, two reading levels across three age tiers": product fact
  (simple and rich narration; early, young, older tiers). Explicit leveling is where
  most AI generators are weak and where leveled readers build parent trust. See the
  landscape notes.
- "in your family's language, properly (en-GB and es-ES, it-IT planned)": product
  fact, and a contrast with the shallow "many languages" breadth of the screen tools.

## What NOT to claim

- Do not say screens or AI "damage", "harm", or "rewire" children's brains. The
  evidence is associational and effect sizes are small.
- Do not cite specific scary statistics about AI and young children. The strong
  AI data is about teens; the young-child evidence is still emerging.
- Do not claim the game "improves IQ", "makes children smarter", or "boosts
  grades". Nothing here supports that, and it is not the point.
- Be honest about AI. The child never interacts with AI, and every story is
  human-written and human-checked, so "no chatbot talking to your child" and "no AI in
  your child's hands" are true and fair. Do not claim "no AI anywhere" or "made without
  AI": the project may use AI tools behind the scenes (for example to draft
  illustrations), always reviewed by a person, and an absolute claim would be untrue.
  Keep the promise about the child's experience, not the workshop.
- The honest, more attractive promise is the soft one: a free, screen-free, no-lose way
  to practise curiosity, imagination, problem-solving, and teamwork together, in your
  family's language, that grows with your child.
