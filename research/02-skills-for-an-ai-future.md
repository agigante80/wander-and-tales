# Theme 2: Skills the next generation needs for an AI-shaped future

What major institutions say children most need to develop now to thrive in an
uncertain, AI-shaped world. This is the most robust and authoritative of the
three themes.

### World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025 [Institutional report] [verified]
Based on a survey of more than 1,000 employers across 55 economies. Employers
expect about 39 percent of workers' core skills to change by 2030. Analytical
thinking is rated the single most essential core skill (around 7 in 10
companies). Creative thinking, resilience/flexibility/agility, and
curiosity/lifelong learning are among the fastest-rising skills, alongside AI and
big-data literacy. The throughline: as routine and analyzable work is
increasingly automated, distinctly human thinking skills rise in value.
https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/
Plain-language summary [Journalism/secondary] [verified]:
https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-jobs-of-the-future-and-the-skills-you-need-to-get-them/

### OECD, Learning Compass 2030 [Institutional report] [verified]
A framework defining the knowledge, skills, attitudes, and values learners need
to fulfil their potential and contribute to their communities. It positions a
broad competency set beyond academic knowledge as essential for future
readiness: creating new value, reconciling tensions and dilemmas, taking
responsibility, and the underlying capacities of reflection, creativity, and
cooperation. In short, the OECD frames adaptability and creative,
responsible thinking, not just facts, as the goal of education.
https://www.oecd.org/en/data/tools/oecd-learning-compass-2030.html

### UNICEF, Global Framework for Transferable Skills [Institutional report] [fetched]
UNICEF's framework for the "transferable" or life skills (sometimes called
21st-century skills or soft skills) that help children learn, find work, and
participate as citizens. It spans cognitive skills (problem-solving, critical
thinking, creativity), social skills (cooperation, communication, empathy), and
self-management (resilience, self-regulation). Useful as a child-focused,
development-oriented counterpart to the employer-focused WEF data.
https://www.unicef.org/reports/global-framework-transferable-skills

## How this connects to the game

The capacities these bodies name most often (analytical and creative thinking,
problem-solving, curiosity, cooperation, resilience, self-regulation) are the
very muscles a cooperative, puzzle-and-story game asks a child to use: decide
what to try, imagine a solution, work with a partner, handle a setback, and stay
curious about what happens next. See `03-play-and-development.md` for the
evidence that play of this kind exercises those muscles.
